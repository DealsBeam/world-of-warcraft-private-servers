September 10, 2026 — BlizzCon 2026 is a hardware story as much as a games story. Diablo 4 hits Switch 2 on September 15. WoW's housing system lands in Midnight 12.1.5. And the emulator community keeps pushing Legion-era clients on weaker hardware than Blizzard ever imagined. Here's what matters for your setup.

## Diablo 4 on Switch 2

Age of Hatred Collection launches September 15 — base game plus both expansions for $69.99. Pre-orders open September 12, BlizzCon Day 1.

The Switch 2 version is a code-in-a-box physical release. That means the cartridge itself holds a download code, not the 90+ GB game. You'll need roughly 90 GB of free storage on your console's SSD or an SD card.

Performance expectations: Diablo 4 runs at 60 FPS docked and 30 FPS handheld on Switch 2, based on the PC requirements and early reports from the Nintendo Direct. Expect reduced texture streaming in handheld mode and some asset pop-in. The game targets the Switch 2's full 1.5 TB/s SSD bandwidth for loading — the code-in-a-box approach means no cartridge loading bottleneck.

## WoW housing and your GPU

Midnight 12.1.5 brings the player housing system and the War Within engine is already a resource hog. Housing districts with lots of decoration can tank frame rates even on midrange GPUs.

The practical question: can you run 12.1.5 on the rig you have? Blizzard's minimum requirements haven't changed (GTX 1060 / RX 580 for 1080p), but housing instances are new territory. If you're planning to make an elaborate housing build, plan for:

- **SSD required.** Housing instances load a lot of unique assets. HDD players will see asset pop-in and long load screens.
- **8 GB VRAM floor for 60 FPS at 1080p with high settings.** Decoration-heavy housing builds can push past the 6 GB VRAM that modern low-end cards carry.
- **CPU matters more than usual.** Housing instances with many players in the same district are CPU-bound. A modern 6-core CPU is the safe floor.

## Legion emulator clients on low-end hardware

The private server scene is livelier than ever for Legion (7.3.5) content — UWoW, UwowSA, and others. The surprising part: Legion clients run better on old hardware than current retail.

Legion's minimum requirements were released in 2016 — GTX 560, 4 GB RAM, i5-2320. Modern Legion private servers run on those same specs without issue because the 7.x client is well-optimized for its era.

**The sweet spot:** a midrange 2016-2018 desktop (GTX 1060 / RX 580, 8-16 GB RAM, any quad-core CPU) runs Legion at 60+ FPS on high settings. That's a $150-200 used machine.

**Steam Deck:** Legion clients run well on Steam Deck with Proton. The 7.3.5 client handles the Deck's 800p resolution and 30 FPS cap easily. Battery life is the constraint — expect 3-4 hours.

## What to buy (or not buy) this weekend

**Skip if you have a 2080-class or newer GPU.** Nothing at BlizzCon 2026 will require more than a 2080-class card for 1440p gaming. Blizzard's games are CPU-tracked, not GPU-bottlenecked.

**Worth buying: an SSD if you're still on HDD.** This is the single biggest upgrade for WoW, Diablo, and any Blizzard game in 2026. Load times drop by 80-90%, asset streaming improves, and housing/group content stutter disappears.

**Worth buying: a Switch 2 dock if you plan to play D4 handheld and docked.** The dock's HDMI 2.1 output lets the Switch 2 do 4K output, and the Switch 2 Pro Controller makes D4 combat less cramped than Joy-Cons.
