Draevor Project announced today, **4 October 2026**, that it is building **Farahlon**: the island northeast of Draenor that Blizzard cut from Warlords of Draenor, the ancient version of what becomes Netherstorm in the main timeline.

## What was announced

Not a quest hub or a custom area, on the project's own description: **"a fully explorable endgame zone, designed to feel like a natural extension of Warlords of Draenor."**

The feature list from the announcement:

- A large explorable island, fully integrated into the Draenor world map
- A new storyline and quest campaign starting with an expedition from Shattrath City
- Iron Horde operations and Blightstone Quarry
- Rares, treasures, bonus objectives, ancient ruins and hidden locations
- Shattrath-to-Farahlon transport

The framing is explicit about intent: "Our goal isn't simply to add another custom zone. Our goal is to expand the unfinished world of Warlords of Draenor and imagine what Farahlon could have become if its development had continued."

## The caveat is printed on the announcement

**"Currently in development. Content and features may change before release."**

That sentence is doing real work and we are leaving it attached to every claim above. This is a dev project announcing a zone, not a live realm shipping one. Draevor's own PTR has been online since late September, and Farahlon has no date at all.

Cut-content restoration is the highest-risk category of private-server announcement, because the reference material does not exist: Blizzard never finished Farahlon, so there is no original to be faithful to and no baseline to check the work against. The announcement is honest about that — "imagine what Farahlon could have become" — and a reader should hold it to invention rather than accuracy.

## Context from the same news feed

The Farahlon post sits atop a feed that has been active for a month: PTR faster-testing update and PTR-online announcement September 26, Garrison mobile app September 25, a first-look PTR post September 25, and patch notes September 19 covering 290+ fixes across all seven Draenor zones.

That cadence is why the announcement is worth publishing despite having no date. A dev project shipping dated notes every few days is demonstrating the capacity to do the thing it just announced, which is more than an announcement alone would establish.
