Blizzard has confirmed the date: **Black Temple arrives on Burning Crusade Anniversary realms August 27**. Illidan Stormrage's fortress — the most iconic raid of the TBC era — opens its gates next Thursday, pushing official Classic players into the expansion's endgame stretch.

## Why this matters beyond the loot table

Two threads converge here. First, TBC Anniversary is visibly accelerating toward its finish: with Black Temple open, the remaining question is Sunwell Plateau — and the handoff after it isn't speculation. Blizzard's own State of Azeroth presentation sequenced it explicitly: Classic news was promised "after The Black Temple," with BlizzCon 2026 ([September 12–13](/news/blizzcon-2026-camelot-private-servers/)) named as the venue. The Lich King's starring role in the show's announcement trailer only reinforced where that news points: **Wrath of the Lich King Anniversary**. Our [Classic+ hub](/classic-plus/) tracks why that timing creates an awkward overlap: leaked timelines put a Wrath Anniversary pre-patch close enough to Camelot that Blizzard's newest Classic offering would compete with its own anniversary progression.

Second, every official anniversary progression squeezes the TBC private server niche a little harder. Players deciding where to spend a leveling month now weigh official realms with fresh phases against projects like [Rising Gods - Back2Basics](/servers/rising-gods-back2basics/) or [Faebright](/servers/faebright/) — each offering different rates, communities and custom twists that official progression can't match.

## The pattern to watch

The anniversary machine has been remarkably consistent: Vanilla Anniversary recycled through 2025–26, TBC Anniversary followed, and Wrath is the obvious next station. For private servers, each official wave resets part of their audience — but history says it doesn't empty the pool. Players who outlevel official content, want different rulesets, or simply prefer smaller communities keep the scene alive between waves.
