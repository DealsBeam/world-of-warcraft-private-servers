The WoW: Forever beta is running a duelling tournament, and it is not Blizzard's idea.

In episode 4 of the official podcast, **Tips Out** described the tournament to Blizzard's own developers: a **community-run** event with money on the line, and a sign-up list that grew far past what its organisers expected.

> "We've had over 6,000 people sign up. We were like expecting maybe 1,000. So things are getting out of control."

He also described where the action is, for anyone logging into the beta tonight:

> "If you want to log in to the beta right now, go outside of Orgrimmar and you'll just see like hundreds of people duelling right now."

## A tradition, not a one-off

The organisers have run this before, and the lineage goes back a long way:

- The first duelling tournament was on the **Classic WoW demo in 2018**, a level 19 event in the Barrens.
- The next was during the **Classic WoW beta in the summer of 2019**.
- The most recent was in **2023**, a **Mak'gora** on the hardcore server.

What changed for Forever is the prize money. The organisers planned a community event and put up prize money of their own. Then, in Tips Out's words, "we didn't expect that Timthetatman was just going to come in and just like double the prize money, which was insane."

Blizzard's own developers were on the receiving end of the scale of it. The episode also contains a line about the beta being a good place for it, with the "unique trinkets", professions and engineering items, consumables, and classes that play "a little bit slower than some of the later versions of the game allow" so a viewer can follow the fight.

## The duel arena was pulled forward

The tournament needs a venue for its later rounds, and Blizzard supplied one inside the beta:

> "This was something that we had kind of planned for later that we went ahead and pulled forward into the beta."

The plan is for **day two of the tournament, for the top 32**, to use a purpose-built duel arena. It is the same kind of decision the Forever team makes repeatedly: a community event needs a space, and the beta is the only place with a full population to test one.

## Why it is worth writing about

A tournament with 6,000 signups inside a beta is a signal about what kind of audience Forever has attracted. It is also the clearest example yet of the beta acting as a venue rather than a test rig, with players using their time to queue for a prize instead of levelling to 30.

Two caveats, stated plainly. The episode was recorded on **15 October**, so the sign-up number is a week old and is almost certainly higher now. And none of this is official Blizzard content. It is a community event that Blizzard's developers were asked about, that Blizzard's beta host, and that a Blizzard-built arena will be used for, and the developers themselves flagged the risk that comes with money on the line: "as the stakes get higher and higher, certain things do become... we have to make sure all of our bases are covered."

## Where to watch it

The tournament runs on community infrastructure with its own stream and its own Discord, and the results and schedule are not published by Blizzard. The beta is open until **10:00 a.m. PDT on Friday, October 23**, and tonight's stress test window is where a very large number of people will be logging in at once. If you want to see the duels rather than read about them, the next two days are the window.
