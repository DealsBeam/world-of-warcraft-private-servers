With Forever's first raids opening **December 9**, the natural question is what hardware they need. The honest answer is that Blizzard has not published raid-specific system requirements, and we will not manufacture a minimum spec.

Our existing hardware piece covers the general floor: on an old machine, storage is the bottleneck. Zone loads on spinning disks run 30 to 60 seconds; on any solid-state drive, under 10. That is a measured observation from our own guide, not a Blizzard number.

## What is actually documented

- The first raid tier is **three raids opening at once**: Barrow Deeps for 10 players, Hyjal Summit for 20 and Onyxia's Lair for 40. Blizzard's October 19 raid overview names all three and the date.
- The live client is **1.60.1.71037** as of November 20.
- Blizzard's November 20 hotfix changed group-finder visibility, so group listing is another part of the raid-night client load.
- The 40-player raid needs the **Drakefire Amulet** chain, and the amulet is per character. That is progression, not hardware.

None of those facts describes a frame rate or a required component.

## What to test before December 9

Rather than guess at a spec, run the same conditions you will raid in:

1. **A full 40-player instance or the busiest capital you can reach.** Open-world crowding in Orgrimmar and Stormwind has been a real load in Forever's first weeks.
2. **A dungeon reset cycle.** Blizzard cut resets from ten per hour to five in the November 20 notes. Repeated zone loads are where a slow drive shows.
3. **Your addon set with everything else disabled.** Blizzard hid realm IDs from addons on November 20, so an older addon may be doing extra work or erroring. Our addon guide explains the checks.
4. **Storage headroom.** Three raids and a live client are not large by modern standards, but a drive near full behaves worse than one with free space. We have no Blizzard figure for required gigabytes.

If those four pass on your current machine, there is no evidence-based reason to buy hardware for December 9.

## What we are not saying

We are not naming a GPU, a CPU, a RAM amount or a minimum internet speed for the raids. Nothing first-party supports a number, and a guide that invents one is worse than a guide that tells you how to measure.
