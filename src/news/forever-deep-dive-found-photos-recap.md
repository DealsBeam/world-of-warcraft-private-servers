September 14, 2026 — Blizzard published its official recaps of the two Saturday WoW: Forever panels: the **Found Photos panel**, which laid out how the team picks new content, and the **Deep Dive**, which detailed systems, classes and the realmless structure. This is the follow-up to our [day-one recap](/news/blizzcon-2026-day-one-recap/) with the verified panel facts.

## TL;DR

- **Found Photos philosophy:** new content must feel like it "may have existed all along" in early Year 1 Azeroth; three buckets — Anticipated, New but Familiar, Unexpected. Term coined by Chris Metzen during Season of Discovery talks.
- **Realmless confirmed:** players pick a ruleset (Normal, PvP, Roleplaying, Hardcore) instead of a realm name. Cross-ruleset grouping unsupported. Two-part names unique per region.
- **Paladin rework detailed:** Holy Strike, Seal of Fury with absorb + Judgment taunt, Consecration baseline at level 20, new Holy/Protection/Retribution spells. Paladins still lack an interrupt and a slow.
- **Six class-race combos confirmed:** Gnome Priest, Human Hunter, Dwarf Shaman, Orc Mage, Troll Warlock, Undead Paladin — plus the Skyborne race split.
- **Legacy points:** earn up to 65 points account-wide, spend up to 16 per character, cosmetic-only reward track (Spectral Bear Cub etc.).
- **Itemization overhaul:** unified hit/crit, weapon skill nerf, new defensive stats, blue-quality unique dungeon boss items, hundreds of new drops.

## The Found Photos panel: how new content gets chosen

The guiding idea, per Blizzard's recap of the panel: content should feel like it was always there, just undiscovered. Forever is set in its own time bubble during early Year 1 Azeroth — before Kazzak reopened the Dark Portal, before Northrend — so new story threads never disturb the foundational era.

Three categories steer the additions:

- **Anticipated:** places players always wanted to reach, like Mount Hyjal.
- **New but familiar:** fresh stories grounded in existing lore, like the Riverglades.
- **Unexpected:** spare, self-contained surprises, like Zephras Isle.

The panel also formalized the **new travel routes** (earlier details in our [hub](/classic-plus/)): Stormwind Harbor to Auberdine, a Menethil-Southshore-Auberdine route, and a Steamwheedle Cartel route from Tanaris to the Riverglades via **Powderfuse Port**. The Riverglades is a mid-level zone around the size of Stranglethorn Vale with 200+ quests, connecting the Burning Steppes, Redridge Mountains, the Swamp of Sorrows and the Badlands. Wetlands gains quests, Desolace's centaur factions get more quests and rewards, and a path south through the Valley of Bones opens the Shen'dralas area.

The Zephras/Skyborne lore got firmer: the Skyborne ("shen'dorei," the hidden people) are rebels who fled Eldre'Thalas after the War of the Ancients and found refuge in Skywall. In Forever their island is failing — the elemental benefactors are gone, the pylons are breaking — and the Windshapers, the High Order and the Al'Aketh all contest control. Forsaken Paladins, rolling out of the Forsaken Kingdom campaign, anchor a story at Bandarion Keep in the Whispering Wood.

## The Deep Dive: systems with concrete numbers

**Realmless servers.** Confirmed structure: choose a ruleset (Normal, PvP, Roleplaying, or Hardcore after launch), not a row of realm names. Each ruleset is a large shared ecosystem; cross-ruleset grouping is not supported, factions stay separate. Hardcore remains closed off except that a dying Hardcore character can transfer out to another ruleset including PvP; nothing flows back in. Characters use two-part names unique per region, and Collections plus Legacy Points are account-wide across rulesets (Hardcore excepted, one-way). PvP rulesets get faction-balance systems in the Season of Discovery mold.

**Legacy and Camping.** The Deep Dive confirmed the [Legacy points economy](/classic-plus/): up to 65 points earnable at launch, each character spends up to 16 across Profession/Adventure/Resourcefulness trees, and the separate reward track is cosmetic-only (Spectral Bear Cub and similar prestige rewards, no gameplay advantage). Challenges are meant to reward how players already play — leveling a class, hitting skill milestones — not to force grind. Camping: the Basic Campfire lets up to three players each add one crafted object (one-hour cooldown), first objects at profession skill 20, advanced Blueprints from dungeon bosses, and cooking upgrades camps to five or ten objects. The Deep Dive named example objects: Blacksmith's Sharpening Wheel (Attack Power), Tailor's Faction Banner (Spirit), Herbalist's Incense Candle (Intellect) — variants that do not stack with class buffs. Alchemists place an Alchemy Lab, leatherworkers a Tanning Rack required for advanced recipes. Forever adds 600+ new recipes across professions, with crafted food granting small XP or stat bonuses.

**Paladin rework.** The most detailed class section of the panel:

- **Holy Strike** at level 6 — instant weapon strike dealing Holy damage on a 12-second cooldown.
- **Seals are no longer consumed** by casting Judgment.
- **Seal of Fury** — tanking seal for fast weapons, small absorb shields, and Judgment becomes an outright taunt while the seal is active.
- **Consecration baseline at level 20** — high damage and threat on the first four targets, falling off beyond that.
- **Holy:** Voice of Truth (temporary silence/interrupt immunity), Reverence (Spirit-based Mana regen while casting), Holy Shock at a 10s cooldown, and Light's Vigil.
- **Protection:** Mana from Seal of Fury absorbs and blocks, a once-per-minute Judgment reset, Templar's Bulwark, and a redesigned Reckoning that also triggers from blocks.
- **Retribution:** Vindication (AP reduction), Sacred Arbiter, Champion of the Light, Instrument of the Law, and Twist of Light enabling seal twisting without a swing-timer add-on.

Blizzard was explicit about limits staying: Paladins still have no interrupt and no slow, and get their taunt through the Seal and Judgment system rather than a single button. They remain strong against Undead and Demons.

**The six new combos.** Officially named in the Deep Dive recap: **Gnome Priest, Human Hunter, Dwarf Shaman, Orc Mage, Troll Warlock, and Undead Paladin**, with more planned. These are on top of the Skyborne faction-class split (Horde Shaman, Alliance Mage) previously flagged from fan transcription of the pre-release build.

**Talent and racial structure.** Talent trees keep Classic rows and the one-point milestones at 11, 21 and 31 points, now joined by a new 16-point milestone. Divine Spirit, Blessing of Kings and Improved Mark of the Wild are baseline for their classes. Racials are restructured to two active and two passive abilities per race; Dwarf Stoneform now reduces Physical damage (not Armor) while removing Bleeds, Poisons and Diseases, and Undead get a weakening Will of the Forsaken (removes Charm/Fear/Sleep, no more immunity), a Mana-restoring Cannibalize, and a new drain-life racial in Touch of the Grave.

**Itemization.** Hit and crit are unified across spell, melee and ranged (helped Warriors, Hunters and Rogues by making Taunts, Poisons and Traps more reliable); weapon skill items grant less per item; bonus healing now includes one third as much bonus damage; some items reduce the chance for attacks to be parried or dodged; spellcaster weapons grant spell damage and healing. Every dungeon drop was re-examined, unique dungeon boss items move to blue quality, set bonuses improved, quest rewards expanded (Mor'ladim's Duskwood quest now adds a caster ring, Hemet Nesingwary's questline adds a spellsword), world-drop epics improved, and hundreds of rare-creature drops added. Trinkets trend situational (the Worgenbane Talisman example). Transmog: bind-on-pickup Uncommon/Rare dungeon drops grant appearances to all eligible looters, epic raid appearances only to the recipient, and poor/common items unlock appearances immediately on loot.
