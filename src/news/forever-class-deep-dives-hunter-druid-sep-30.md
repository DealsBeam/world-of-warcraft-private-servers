Blizzard has published the first of its **World of Warcraft: Forever class deep dives**, two on 30 September, covering **Hunter and Druid** and **Priest and Warrior**. Both carry the same explicit warning that the numbers will keep moving during beta.

The detail that matters is structural, and it is identical in both posts.

## The 16-point milestone

Original WoW gave talents in rows with one-point milestone talents at **11, 21 and 31 points**. Both deep dives say the same thing:

> "Starting at level 10, talents become available and keep the familiar structure, including the same number of rows and one-point milestone talents at 11, 21, and 31 points, now joined by a new 16-point milestone."

One post adds detail the other does not: some talents "remain exactly as they were in patch 1.12 (from original World of Warcraft)", while others were adjusted, and class buffs that used to come from talents, **Blessing of Kings and Improved Mark of the Wild**, are now baseline.

Also confirmed in both: talents are changed at a **class trainer in a major city**, and **Dual Specialization unlocks at level 40**.

## Hunter

Baseline **Aimed Shot at level 20**, traps usable in combat, and pets gaining definition through "Hunter gear scaling, broader ability access, and unique family abilities", including **Foxes as a new pet family**.

The three specs are described as pet-focused play, ranged attacks, and trap and melee options. The framing is flexibility plus identity as "a ranged combatant with a trusted companion at its side".

## Druid

The post covers adaptable forms and nature magic, and is paired with the Hunter post as the first of a series covering every class.

## Priest

**Fear Ward and Devouring Plague available to all Priests at level 20, regardless of race. Divine Spirit baseline at level 30. Shadow Word: Death joins the class as a baseline ability at level 32.**

That last one is the most consequential line in either post, because Shadow Word: Death is a level-40-era ability arriving at 32, which is an earlier and more available copy of the class's identity spell than original WoW offered.

Priest racials were "fully re-evaluated", and the post states that **"Each race of Priest has two spells unique to their Race."**

## Warrior

The post describes adjustments that "make Rage more reliable, bring certain utility forward as baseline functionality, and sharpen the role of each specialization", covering **weapons, Rage, stances and battlefield momentum**, with changes across Arms, Fury and Protection.

## What to hold and what not to

These are design notes published while the beta is running, and both posts say so: *"Please note that class abilities and talents may continue to evolve throughout the beta test and are subject to additional changes."*

That is the whole reason we do not turn them into a build guide. A talent tree described on 30 September is evidence of direction, not of the tree you will play on 4 November, and we have no beta client to check any of it against. We will treat these as dated readings and revisit them if the numbers change.

The remaining classes have not been covered yet. Two posts covering four classes is a deliberate rollout, so the interesting question is whether the 16-point milestone holds for the other six or whether it is a Hunter and Priest decision.
