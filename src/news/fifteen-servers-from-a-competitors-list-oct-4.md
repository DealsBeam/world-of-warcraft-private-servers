A competing tracker, privateserverhub.com, publishes a server list with per-project pages that name each project's website. We diffed their 75 pages against our 149 entries. **Sixteen were missing from ours.** One turned out to be ours already. Fourteen are new entries as of today, all verified first-party rather than copied from their pages.

Their pages were used for one thing only: **finding the project URLs**. Every fact in our entries comes from reading the project sites ourselves, because a competitor's research is a lead, not a source.

## The one that was already ours

Their **Duskhaven Reforged** page points at **duskhaven.net**, which is our existing Duskhaven entry's address. Same Vanilla+ project, same missing launch date. Not a new server.

Our entry now notes it is also listed under the fuller name, and keeps `dev` since their page describes a closed PTR we could not verify first-party behind the site's Cloudflare challenge.

One of the sixteen was evaluated and **not added**. Mythic Dawn has no website, only a Discord invite, and creating an entry with no URL would manufacture exactly the kind of sourceless record this tracker has spent a week eliminating. It stays out until it has an address.

## The dated claim: Nozdor's DUNE opens October 16

Nozdor's front page announces a new world called **DUNE opening 16 October**, with no year stated. Read in context it is the coming October 16.

That is now the **third dated private-server claim** on our calendar alongside UwowSA's October 10 content drop and uWoW's October 16 realm opening. Two launches on the same day, one Russian and one international. We will re-check all three on their dates and publish the results either way.

## Hour of Twilight finally has an address

One of our six never-dated dev entries just got its first URL. **houroftwilight.net** resolves and lands on the project's Discord invite, which read **5,291 members with 1,688 online**.

Twelve guessed domains across two batches found nothing because the project lives at `.net` and every guess was `.com`. The entry keeps `dev` with the Discord figures recorded as their widget, and a third-party description of an ARPG-style project in testing since 2021 is noted as their claim.

## Their closed list versus our entries

Their site publishes a closed list with dates and reasons. It makes claims about seven of our entries, and we have recorded each as their claim, not as our finding:

- **Classic Plus, Feenix, Galaxyofdrone, GryffinWow, True Azeroth, Valanior**: listed closed, undated. Converges with our dead domains but supplies no date, so all six stay as they are rather than dead.
- **Dalaran-WoW**: listed closed, undated. **Conflicts** with the administered 503 page we read October 3. Both recorded, neither dated, rating kept.

Five closed projects on their list that we never tracked (Firemaw, Warborn, WotLKPlus, Best, Forgotten Land) **are not added**, because none carries a shutdown date and our dead-entry gate requires one. A dateless dead entry is exactly the kind of value this tracker refuses to publish.

## What we are taking from their operation, stated openly

They publish an llms.txt, regenerated hourly, with their ranking formula disclosed: reviews 50%, estimated population 35% on a log scale, years online 15%. They publish aggregates per expansion, a closed list with successors, and a monthly small-server vote. Those are good ideas, stated as theirs, and worth learning from rather than copying. Our own llms.txt exists but is thinner, which is now a queued improvement.
