September 17, 2026 — a BlizzCon 2026 interview gives the clearest first-party read yet of WoW Forever's two headline systems: the campsite rules that decide where and how long you can camp, and the biome-specific item effects that underpin horizontal progression. Senior Game Designer **Josh Greenfield** and Senior Art Director **Jeff Parrott** talked with MMORPG.com's Robin Baird (September 15); MassivelyOP's recap the following day independently covers the same ground.

## Campsites: placement, duration and the anti-gatekeeping rule

- Campfires and their fixtures last **about 10 minutes**, and each placement sits on a **one-hour cooldown** — corroborating the one-hour cooldown in wowforeverclassic's beta demo read
- Almost anywhere except cities, with a **minimum distance between fires** ("we don't want shanty towns to pop up"); basic campfires hold three fixtures and higher-level camps hold more
- Camps are **level-restricted** and cheap to enter
- A better object **replaces** a weaker one: "if they put the better sharpening stone down, it'll just replace the rinky dink one" — so no profession can gate camps behind an elite build. Playtests saw players "just kind of happy to get a buff," and the team has "levers and knobs" if the camp meta "gets out of control"
- A "you smell an inviting fire" buff points players toward nearby campsites — and placement choices can be lethal: a dev's campfire on the Duskwood road got her killed by Stitches

Greenfield hedges the 10-minute duration with "I think," so treat the timers as developer estimates until the beta client confirms them. The full object list lives on the [Forever hub](/classic-plus/).

## Biome-specific gear: horizontal progression's engine

Greenfield confirmed itemization's horizontal layer is **biome-specific effects** — "hundreds of those" keyed to activities and threats. Examples: **Whitemane's Chapeau** makes resurrection spells cast faster, and sets grant more damage against dragons, so a set can matter long past its tier — you might "put together the best dragon-slaying set" by pugging a raid tier from two years earlier.

Raid tiers are tuned for **incremental, nominal power gains** instead of dramatic jumps, learning from how Naxx-era vanilla optimized the game dry. Epics "maintain that prestige," says Greenfield, while a blue item can still be "better for a specific purpose" than an epic. That intent matches the confirmed chase-drops design: every boss keeps low-drop, very strong items so old raids stay relevant.

Storage grows to support pack-rat play: the default bank is much larger — "96 slots or something like that... either twice or four times as large" — while bags start at the original sizes, with more storage options planned down the road (those numbers are dev estimates, hedged the same way).

## Also clarified

- **Transmog toggle** — turning transmog off only hides what you see on other players; your own configured outfit stays visible. Forever still won't let you hide your helmet or cloak, keeping the vanilla rule.
- **Class quest lines** — Season of Discovery's class quests carried over, some new, some expanded, with more on the whiteboard for after launch.
- **World changes** — additive-not-destructive is the design goal; situational terrain changes are possible but kept "sparing," with Cataclysm's ambition-as-cost cited as the cautionary tale (Azshara gained quests, Wetlands got new lighting).
- **Pacing** — leveling combat stays Classic-paced: around levels 10–15 a kill takes 10–15 seconds, and pulling three means "you're probably gonna die."
- **Built-in UI** - the damage meter is in; ~~the cooldown manager is planned for the near future and may not make beta~~. **Update September 25:** Blizzard's official beta notes now describe a limited Cooldown Manager for five classes, disabled by default, with spell-rank support still pending.

The item-surprise design Greenfield described in this same interview — item stats hidden until an item first drops on your server, and no Dungeon Journal explanations at launch — is already covered in our [beta day-one coverage](/news/forever-beta-live-sep-17/).
