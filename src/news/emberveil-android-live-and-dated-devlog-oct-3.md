Emberveil was last checked on **25 August** and we recorded its Android build as "closed beta signups open, invites pending". That was true then and it understates what happened next. The client now **runs**.

## What the devlog says, newest first

**15 September, "One Month of Emberveil"**, celebrating the realm's first month: **340+ bug reports closed, 97 client revisions**, a **nine hour DDoS weathered**, and "put the client on a phone".

**5 September, "We put Emberveil on a phone. Now we need it broken."** The client **runs on Android on its own test realm**, with closed beta applications open, **invites going out in waves and the first wave deliberately small**. The title is the whole development philosophy in a sentence: they shipped it to a device they do not own and asked for it to break.

**20 August, addon directory open.** Submissions are reviewed before publishing, and "the download always comes from the same repository as the code". That is a supply-chain detail most projects in this scene do not make.

**19 August, Lua API documentation live**, and the post notes it is "useful even if you are building an addon for the original 1.12.1 game", which is a deliberate nod to the wider Classic addon ecosystem rather than a walled garden.

**15 August, launch party** with Out Of Bounds Radio, five DJs and the station playing in game from the moment the realm opened.

**13 August, client download open** with character nickname reservations.

**6 August, writing contest**, Season 1 titled **"Echoes of the Ashes"**, asking for stories about life in Azeroth after the Third War, with **winning entries becoming part of the official lore** and the best ideas potentially inspiring future quests, NPCs and world events.

## Why this one is different from most entries in this tracker

Most of what we track is a feature list. This is a **devlog with dates, numbers and a stated intent**, and two of those numbers are the kind of thing operators normally omit.

The **nine hour DDoS** is one. A month-old realm taking a nine hour attack and reporting it in a celebratory post rather than burying it is either confidence or inexperience, and the same post reports 340 bug reports closed against 97 client revisions, which is a ratio that suggests they are shipping faster than they are stabilising.

The **writing contest feeding into official quests** is the other. It is a stated intention, not a shipped feature, and we have no way to check whether quests actually came from it. Worth watching rather than crediting.

## The underlying claim

The site's own framing: classic **patch 1.12.1 running inside a fully functional Unreal Engine 5 client**, "the thing people swore couldn't be done". Same quests, same dungeons, same roads, on an engine twenty years younger.

That is the project's whole proposition and it is a **UE5 rebuild**, which makes Emberveil the most interesting engineering entry we track. It also means it sits in a category where our evidence is weakest: we are describing what the site says about a technical achievement, and we have not run the client. The launcher is a **7z archive** and we did not download or execute it.

Also live alongside the devlog: a **Dungeon Finder and Group Browser**, a **database browser at its own subdomain**, an **armory**, an addon wiki, and locale paths for **Spanish, Russian and Simplified Chinese**.

## What has not changed

**The devlog's newest post is dated 15 September**, which is 18 days ago. Nothing has shipped publicly in that window, and for a project whose headline is a monthly engineering pace, an 18-day silence is the first thing worth watching.

Our population tier stays `tiny` on the basis of the site's own positioning as a small realm. We have no live counter from it.
