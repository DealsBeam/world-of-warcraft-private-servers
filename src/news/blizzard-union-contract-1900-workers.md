September 9, 2026 — Blizzard union members represented by the Communications Workers of America (CWA) ratified a contract covering 1,900 workers across World of Warcraft, Diablo, Overwatch, Hearthstone & Warcraft Rumble, Story & Franchise Development, Platform & Technology, and Quality Assurance. The contract extends to all unionized units under a single agreement, a first for Blizzard.

## What the contract covers

**Layoff protections.** A minimum of four weeks of severance plus one additional week for every six months of employment, up to 39 weeks maximum. Workers also won 14-month recall rights that span across units — a laid-off WoW employee can be recalled into an open Diablo or Overwatch position if qualified. "This has definitely never happened in our industry before," WoW senior game designer Paul Cox told Aftermath.

**Just cause protections.** Employees can no longer be terminated without documented reason. "We have finally entered the industrialized world where most countries have these kinds of protections," Diablo senior environment artist Mahreen Fatima told Aftermath.

**Hybrid and remote work locked in.** Current hybrid schedules are contractually guaranteed. Fully remote workers are grandfathered in and cannot be forced into an office they never worked at. Every other Activision studio is back four to five days per week.

**Salary floors.** Ranges between $51,840 and $70,400 depending on location. The highest individual raise from the contract is 34%, though workers noted pay was the area where they compromised most. "We'd hoped that we would get more pay," Cox said.

**AI protections.** The company must bargain with the union before implementing AI technology that affects working conditions. AI will be discussed regularly as part of labor-management committee meetings. "Some teams and projects are experimenting and leaning into AI more than others," Fatima said. "The AI language we got gives us a seat at the table."

## How it happened

Workers had been bargaining for over two years. Units beyond WoW found themselves stuck with few bargaining dates while WoW negotiated first. The breakthrough came in August 2026 when workers proposed extending WoW's contract terms to all units rather than bargaining separately. Blizzard agreed, and workers spent roughly 70 hours in a conference room over five days to finalize the deal.

The timing mattered. Workers had been protesting outside Blizzard's 12-foot orc statue on the Irvine campus "on a regular basis," senior editor Alison Veneto told Aftermath. "Going into BlizzCon without a contract with the level of mobilization that had been ramping up from Blizzard employees could have be uncomfortable."

Microsoft's plan to cut another 1,600 Xbox employees within the next year made layoff protections the top priority. Blizzard mostly dodged the most recent round of cuts, but the contract provides a safety net if future rounds hit.

## What it means for BlizzCon

The contract was ratified three days before BlizzCon 2026 opens on September 12. Workers can now focus on the show without the distraction of ongoing negotiations. "Now that the contract is ratified, this is not the end," Veneto said. "We don't just take a nap now, because we have to enforce the contract."
