Buried in the Priest section of Blizzard's **30 September** class deep dive is the largest single deviation from original WoW in either published post:

> "Shadow Word: Death joins the class as a baseline ability at level 32."

In original WoW, **Shadow Word: Death is a level-40 ability**. It arrives here at **32**, and it is baseline, meaning every Priest gets it rather than choosing it.

## Why eight levels is a lot

A Shadow Priest's damage identity in original WoW is built on a slow ramp: a DoT that does nothing meaningful in low levels, then becomes the class's primary burst and execute at 40, with the talent **Improved Shadow Word: Death** as a milestone reward for committing.

Moving it to 32 as baseline changes three things at once:

- **The ramp is eight levels shorter.** The window where Shadow is waiting for its payoff shrinks by a fifth of a level-60 character's life.
- **It is no longer a choice.** A Discipline or Holy Priest now has a baseline execute they will almost never use, and the Shadow spec no longer has a talent row to spend on it.
- **The 40-level spike lands on a different ability.** Whatever used to gate progression at 40 now has to come from something else.

The third point is the one nobody can answer yet, and it is the question a returning Shadow Priest should be asking. If the answer is "the 16-point milestone", then the 16-point milestone is not a cosmetic addition, it is the compensation for this.

## The rest of the Priest changes, for context

The same post also gives **Fear Ward** and **Devouring Plague** to all Priests at level **20**, regardless of race, and **Divine Spirit** as baseline at level **30**. That is a coordinated loosening: three spells across three level gates, all baseline, all race-independent.

Priest racials were "fully re-evaluated", with **each race having two spells unique to their race**. The post does not enumerate them in the text we read, so we are not going to list classes' racials we have not seen.

## The honest limit

**This is a design post read on 2 October, 33 days before launch.** It says explicitly that abilities "may continue to evolve throughout the beta test and are subject to additional changes". A single ability's level gate is exactly the kind of number that moves during a beta, and it moved once already: the same post's own baseline list shows Blizzard actively moving spells between talents and baseline, so the direction of travel is not yet settled.

We are recording it because it is the most surprising number in the two deep dives, and because a reader who starts planning a Shadow Priest at level 20 on the strength of this should know it might be 40 when the game launches.
