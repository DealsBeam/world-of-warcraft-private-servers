Blizzard published a detailed breakdown of the **Legacy System** on **23 September 2026**. It is the most mechanically specific thing Blizzard has published about Forever outside the class deep dives, and the detail that decides whether the system is generous or stingy is a single number: **16 points per character at launch**.

## What it is

An **account-wide horizontal progression system** with three parts: **Legacy Challenges, Legacy Perks, Legacy Rewards**. The stated goal is "exploring everything the game has to offer, rather than power progression alone", and it explicitly does not require alts: *"leveling alternate characters is not required to benefit from the system. All character advantages can be unlocked on a single character."*

Access comes with your **first Legacy Point**, normally when your first character hits **level 25**, or by reaching **150** in a non-gathering primary tradeskill, or by exploring the whole world map. Opened via the shield icon on the menu or the default hotkey **Y**.

## 65 challenges, six categories

Each completed challenge earns **one Legacy Point**, and points are **Battle.net account-wide**, usable by all non-Hardcore characters.

| Category | Points | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classes | 27 | Each class to level 25, 45 and 60 |
| Tradeskills | 18 | Each non-gathering primary to 150, 225 and 300 |
| Player vs Player | 12 | Ranks 3, 7, 10, 13, 14 (5); Exalted with each Battleground rep (4); weeks 4, 7 and 10 of the Field of Honor season (3) |
| Adventure | 2 | Full world map on one character; the Lord Valthalak dungeon set questline |
| Dungeons | 3 | Final boss in every 15-25, 26-45 and 46-60 dungeon |
| Raids | 3 | Onyxia, all 13 Hyjal Summit bosses, all 8 Barrow Deeps bosses |

That is **65 challenges**. Note the arithmetic the post does not spell out: 27 from classes alone needs nine classes levelled, which is the alts the system says you do not need.

## The cap, and why it exists

Blizzard is explicit about the reasoning, which is unusually candid:

> "At the launch of the game, you will be able to spend up to 16 points total on each character. This constraint solves a couple big issues. First, new players will not be so far behind established players that they can't catch up. Second, 16 points can be reached on a single character, so it is not required to create alts."

The second claim is checkable against their own table. A single character can earn **3 points from levelling, up to 6 from tradeskills, 12 from PvP, 2 from Adventure, and 6 from dungeons and raids, for up to 29** on one character, against a cap of 16 to spend. So a solo player can out-earn the cap without a single alt, which is what Blizzard says, and can also hit the ceiling before collecting a third of what is available.

The honest reading is that **the cap is a new-player fairness mechanic and it is tight**. An established account that levels nine classes to 45 will have banked 27 points against a 16-point spend limit, and the surplus goes to waste on any character it has not levelled yet.

## What you can buy

Points buy advantages per character in **Professions, Adventure and Resourcefulness**, and each character spends independently.

A sample from the posts: **Working Overtime** (chance to gain a skill point in all tradeskills including Lockpicking), **Bountiful Harvest** (more Scarce resources from mining, herbing, skinning), **Bartering** (lower vendor prices), **Master Chef**, **Luremaster**, **Dedicated Study** (daily cooldown). In Adventure: **Well Rested** (raises the rested XP cap and accumulates faster), **Field Guide** (cuts the 1-hour camping feature cooldown), **Talented** (unlock talent points up to **5 levels lower** than normal), **Frequent Flier** (cheaper and faster flights). In Resourcefulness: **Gourmand**, **The Quick and the Dead**.

Two new factions are named as sources of tradeskill activity: the **Azeroth Commerce Authority** and **Durotar Supply and Logistics**.

## What we cannot tell you

**Legacy Rewards is not explained in this post.** The three components are named and two are covered in depth, and the third is not, so we are not going to guess what it is or whether it is the account-wide catch that would change the whole economy of the cap.

We also cannot confirm any of it in game. This is a design post read on 2 October, **33 days from launch**, and a cap that tight may well be one of the numbers that moves before 4 November.
