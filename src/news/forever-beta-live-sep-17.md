September 17, 2026 — the **WoW: Forever beta goes live today**: Blizzard confirmed the servers open right after the Forever Q&A segment (10:30 a.m. PDT start), per WoW Classic's official Reddit post. Two more systems got developer confirmation this week — a **one-faction lock on the PvP mega-server** and **surprise-loot itemization** — both now on the [hub](/classic-plus/).

## Beta logistics, today

Access still comes through the [Skyborne Epic Pack or Warcraft Forever Collection](/news/forever-pre-purchase-editions-beta/), or a free opt-in at the WoW Forever website that is **not guaranteed** (random picks). Nothing from the beta carries to launch: November 4 is a clean slate, no advantages or rewards transfer, per Blizzard's beta framing. Level cap and window — September 17 to October 21 — stay as [listed](/news/forever-beta-verification-hit-list/). The [verification hit list](/news/forever-beta-verification-hit-list/) is the tracker's priority sheet for day-one datamining; the [Q&A question post](/news/wow-forever-live-qa-questions/) covers what we still want answered on stream today.

## New: PvP mega-server = one faction only

In a Game Informer interview, Blizzard confirmed that **on the PvP ruleset mega-server you are restricted to one faction** — Horde *or* Alliance, not both, the pre-Patch-3.2 rule. The stated reasons: keep the mega-server feeling like Horde vs. Alliance, stop players hopping factions to scout and report enemy groups, and protect faction balance — with the Deep Dive's SoD-style pledge of "systems intended to support Horde and Alliance balance," most likely temporary character-creation restriction for the dominant faction.

Comfort for the private-server crowd: that is exactly the faction-locked dynamic the tracker's Vanilla/TBC-era realms run by default. The difference is scale — Forever's PvP ruleset is one shared ecosystem, not dozens of realm shards.

## New: loot stays a surprise

Senior Game Designer Josh Greenfield told MMORPG.com that an item's stats only become visible **when it first drops on the server** — for you *or* anyone — and that the Dungeon Journal will initially leave encounters unexplained. The goal is discovery over pre-built BiS lists: players learn new loot and raid mechanics together, the way SoD phase launches briefly felt.

Pair this with the [already-confirmed itemization](/classic-plus/) — chase drops on every boss, slowed progression, "everything at 60 stays relevant" — and Forever's loot reads as an anti-datamine stance from day one. Three direct consequences worth watching in beta: dataminers fight a client that hides its own tables, first-clear races become loot-reveal races, and community wikis start from observed drops, not extracted data.

## Why it matters

Today is the tracker's busiest Forever day since the reveal: the Q&A answers the [hit list](/news/forever-beta-verification-hit-list/) questions live, the beta opens immediately after, and the first real (not demo-captured) server data starts flowing.
