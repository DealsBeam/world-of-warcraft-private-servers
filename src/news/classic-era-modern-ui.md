Blizzard quietly shipped one of the more telling updates of the year to its oldest game versions: **Edit Mode and 41-yard nameplates are now live on Classic Era, Hardcore and Season of Discovery**. The rollout completed in late July, and it's worth pausing on — because both features have a history that runs straight through the private server scene.

## Nameplates: from gray-area addon to official feature

Extended nameplate distance has been a Classic community fixture for as long as Classic has existed — first through addons that stretched the default range, later through tweaks Blizzard alternately tolerated and stamped out. Forty-one yards (the maximum interact distance) is exactly what those tools delivered. Building it into the base client ends a two-decade arms race between players wanting better combat readability and a client enforcing 2004 defaults.

Edit Mode is the same story at larger scale: retail's drag-and-drop HUD customization, ported onto the 1.15.x Classic branch. The old world now ships with new-world ergonomics.

## The convergence thesis, confirmed again

This is the pattern we documented in [the modern QoL wave](/news/modern-qol-classic-cores/): the line between "old WoW" and "modern WoW" keeps blurring, from both directions at once. Private servers port Hero Talents, Warbands-style account systems and playerbots onto classic cores; meanwhile official Classic absorbs exactly the conveniences that made private servers attractive. Dual spec, group finder, and now full HUD control — Era is drifting toward the QoL baseline that used to be a private-server selling point.

It also sharpens the question hanging over September's BlizzCon: if Project Camelot is real ([all current evidence here](/classic-plus/)), an official Classic+ would presumably launch with these modern systems baked in from day one. Private servers would then compete on what official Classic won't do — custom zones, class reworks, bot populations — rather than on quality of life.

## Purists vs convenience

The blizzlike end of the scene isn't thrilled — x1 purist realms market themselves partly on the *absence* of these comforts, and some Era communities treat Edit Mode as heresy by patch notes. But the player numbers suggest most people want the old world without the old friction. Both products keep growing; what's shrinking is the middle ground pretending otherwise.
