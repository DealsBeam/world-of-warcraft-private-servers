title: "Forever's final class deep dive rewrites how Paladin Seals work"
date: 2026-10-20
category: news
summary: "Blizzard closed the nine-class series on October 15. Judgement no longer eats the active Seal, Consecration is capped at four targets for threat, and Seal Twisting is removed from the baseline and returned as a Retribution capstone."
---

Blizzard published the **ninth and final** World of Warcraft: Forever class deep dive on **October 15 2026**, and it is the Paladin. The post opens by saying the series "concludes" with this installment, so every class now has an official developer breakdown.

The headline changes are structural rather than cosmetic, and two of them touch the parts of the class that Classic players care about most.

## Seals are no longer one-button

Three baseline changes do most of the work:

- **Judgement no longer consumes the Paladin's active Seal.** That removes the timing tax on every Judgement and is the single biggest quality change in the post.
- **Salvation is no longer a Blessing.** A Paladin can now hold a Blessing on a target and Salvation at the same time, and the raid-wide class version is renamed **Mass Salvation**.
- **Holy Strike** is a new baseline ability learned at **level 6**, dealing instant Holy damage based on 50% of weapon damage plus a rank-scaling bonus.

**Seal of Fury** is a new seal at **level 10** with three components: constant bonus Holy damage per melee swing, which the post notes "favors using a fast weapon while tanking"; an absorption shield equal to 50% of that damage, which does not stack; and a Judgement that deals extra Holy damage and **taunts the target**, with a longer 10-yard range than Growl or Taunt.

## Consecration is deliberately capped

Consecration moves from a Holy talent to a **level 20 baseline ability for all Paladins**, and it is redesigned to generate threat on only **the first four enemies** to enter it, with "very low damage to all other enemies".

Blizzard is explicit about why, and it is the clearest design statement in the whole series: this limitation is "critical to maintaining the combat feel of Classic Era, and not sliding into the more AoE-focused gameplay found in early WoW expansions". Protection also loses some threat, with **Righteous Fury's** bonus reduced because the spec now has more Holy damage than Classic Era.

Two smaller baseline notes: resistance auras now cover all raid members within 30 yards, and **Lay on Hands** drops from a 1 hour cooldown to **20 minutes**.

## Seal Twisting is now a talent

In Classic Era, Seal Twisting was a timing trick available to everyone. Forever **removes it from the baseline** and returns it as **Twist of Light**, the Retribution **31-point gold medal talent**, described as retaining the gameplay "while making it more intuitive and less stringent on timing windows".

Holy gets the alternative: **Holy Shock** moves from the 31-point slot to the **21-point** slot, so a Paladin can opt out of Seal Twisting entirely and build a hybrid around Holy instead.

## What changed in the Holy tree

The post lists a full pass, and a few items matter beyond Paladins:

- **Voice of Truth** is a new 11-point gold medal ability: 6 seconds of immunity to Silence and Interrupt on a 3 minute cooldown.
- **Reverence** is a new row 3 talent granting up to 30% of mana regeneration from Spirit while casting.
- **Purifying Power** cuts Cleanse and Purify mana by up to 20% and Exorcism and Holy Wrath cooldowns by up to 33%.
- **Infusion of Light** makes Holy Shock and Flash of Light crits reduce the next Holy Light cast by up to 1 second.
- **Illumination** now requires Reverence, can also trigger from Light's Vigil, and refunds **50%** of the base cost instead of the old effect, because it "scaled out of control".
- **Divine Favor** moves from the 21-point to the **16-point** gold medal, and a new **16-point** slot joins the familiar 11, 21 and 31.
- **Divine Precision** grants up to **18% increased hit chance with Holy spells**, because Blizzard does "not plan to include itemization for Hit chance on Plate healing gear".

Talent rules stay stable: talents open at **level 10**, dual specialization at **40**, and resets happen at a class trainer in a major city.

## What it means

Three patterns run through this post and the eight before it. Baseline abilities that were once talent-locked are being handed out earlier. Sharp edges that produced high-variance play are being gated behind capstones or capped outright. And convenience that used to be gear-dependent, like spell hit for Holy, is being moved into talents so the gear can stay thematic.

Blizzard attaches the usual caveat: abilities and talents "may continue to evolve throughout the beta test", and the Salvation change is explicitly "not yet live" and going into a future build. With the beta ending on **October 21**, that future build is the launch one.
