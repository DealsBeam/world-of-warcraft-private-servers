September 15, 2026 — Kotaku's August 14 post-BlizzCon interview with WoW lead designers Tim Jones (Classic) and Michael Nuthals (encounter design) surfaces eight key design decisions that answer several open questions heading into Thursday's beta.

### No Season-of-Discovery-style role combos

Kotaku leads with the headline that drew the most pre-beta buzz: "no Shaman Tanks Will Be Coming to World of Warcraft: Forever." Jones describes Forever's range as "a wide range of abilities and talents" but "not to the level of complexity that's going to be seen in the Season of Discovery rune system" — the baseline is Classic-era class structure with meaningful talent trees, not the DPS-Hunter or Tank-Mage hybrids that SoD introduced. Mage healing and Shaman tanking are out for launch.

### Every raid tier ships a 10p + 20p, sometimes 40p

The standard raid offering is one 10-player and one 20-player option per tier, with a 40-player tier coming "every once in a while." Jones frames it as the same permanent structure going forward: "a 10-player raid for groups that want a smaller challenge and a 20-player raid for the groups that are a little bit more ambitious." No flex raiding.

### Meeting stones = Looking for Group, no summon

Meeting Stones still exist and players can interact with them to open a Looking for Group tool, but they do not summon anyone. You still need a Warlock to summon — or walk.

### Mount training gold reworked

Jones says the team moved the gold cost from the mount to the riding training itself. "We're not giving someone free training for a mount when they hit level 40." The "effort-focused" framing confirms our Cerulean Prideclaw pre-purchase note that the mount is cosmetic; the gated part is the skill.

### Add-on restrictions mirror retail

Nuthals describes "in-game or in-combat add-on disarmament" similar to retail WoW, especially targeting "computational add-ons" in PvP. Nuthals notes "there is a really high barrier to entry if you want to get good at PvP in WoW," and that one of the team's design goals is to help close that gap.

### No one-button rotation

Nuthals carries forward the August statement on rotation-macro support: "I think it's kind of really important that everyone is actively pressing the buttons they want to press. That level of investment is something we're really interested in keeping and we want to make sure we don't lose sight of that."

### PvP honor progression to rank 14

Jones describes a Classic-style honor system that leads to rank 14, but designed to fit within a season or patch cycle rather than the years-long grind of original Vanilla. Rewards include cosmetic rank items plus PvP armor with stats. The Darkspear Islands battleground (15v15) and original Classic battlegrounds both play at launch.

### "Forever" = a safe-space promise

Jones describes the word Forever as "a safe space, it's legacy, it's everything that we've made so far is going to matter and impact your alts and the game modes that come out of it going forward. It's always evolving, it's always moving forward."

### Storefront details: Epic $59.99, Collection $79.99, free beta opt-in

The Kotaku interview answers a cluster of purchasing questions that the official Blizzard announcements left open. GameSpot's September 15 beta-access guide independently confirms three more: the Skyborne Epic Pack is listed at **$59.99 / £51.99**, the Warcraft Forever Collection at **$79.99 / £69.99**, and a free beta opt-in exists at the WoW Forever website — GameSpot calls the opt-in a "sign-up" that does not guarantee a seat. The beta's end date of October 21 is also independently confirmed by GameSpot's guide (we flagged the one-day discrepancy with the roadmap graphic's October 22 in [the hub](/classic-plus/)).

### Talent calculator + dual spec

The fan-run [talentsforever.com](https://talentsforever.com), transcribed from BlizzCon demo footage, confirms 51 talent points across the classic 31-point-style trees (rows open every 5 points) with a **dual-spec** feature coming - the Secondary tab was present but locked in the demo. The calculator notes its tooltips are "Estimated" and cites both the BlizzCon demo and Wowhead's Legacy System Explained article (September 12) as sources. ~~The Legacy perks count there is 19 across Adventure, Resourcefulness and Professions, versus the 17 transcribed by classicwowforever.com~~ was a September 15 discrepancy. Blizzard's September 23 official article now names **21 perks**, seven in each tree, so the old count is superseded.
