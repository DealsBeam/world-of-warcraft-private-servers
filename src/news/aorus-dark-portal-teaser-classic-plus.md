September 11, 2026 — One day before BlizzCon, a hardware sponsor's deleted teaser is doing more Classic+ speculation work than a month of leak threads. Here is the verification ledger: confirmed, single-source, and pure speculation, separated.

## TL;DR

- AORUS posted a BlizzCon teaser September 8 showing a darkened, apparently closed Dark Portal under "All Eyes on Azeroth". The posts were later deleted.
- VERIFIED: GIGABYTE is an official BlizzCon 2026 sponsor, so the teaser is plausibly legit rather than fan art.
- SINGLE-SOURCE: the deletion itself is reported by one outlet only. Treat the "why" as unknown.
- The private-server read: a closed portal points at an Azeroth-only Classic+, which would leave Outland — and TBC-era servers — untouched.

## The teaser: what we know

Icy Veins reports that AORUS, the gaming brand of GIGABYTE, posted the teaser across its brand accounts on September 8. The image carries the official BlizzCon logo, shows the Dark Portal with no glow, and promises "something special is coming to BlizzCon".

Two alternative explanations compete. One: the closed portal is deliberate pictorial language — Classic+ stays on Azeroth instead of walking through to Outland. Two: it is just hardware promo art with a Warcraft skin, and the portal means nothing at all.

## What checks out

Blizzard's own sponsor thank-you page lists GIGABYTE alongside Dell, MSI, NVIDIA, Fanta, and Secretlab. A sponsor posting BlizzCon-branded teasers is normal business, which cuts both ways: the image is likely real marketing, but real marketing for hardware tells us nothing about game content.

Independent corroboration of the discussion exists. A September 11 community thread argues a "Second War Classic+" reading fits the teaser better than a Chromie time-travel what-if. That confirms the teaser is circulating and being dissected — not what it means.

The "Warcraft Forever" name trail also still holds. The PTR bindings file with legacy bars, the insider forum line, and the September 2 domain registration all predate this teaser, and a fresh RDAP lookup reconfirms the domain: registered September 2 through Cloudflare, expiring September 2027. The caveat stands — Blizzard usually registers core domains through MarkMonitor, not Cloudflare. Full breakdown lives in the [Warcraft Forever triple-signal post](/blog/warcraft-forever-triple-signal/).

## What it means for private servers

If Classic+ genuinely keeps its eyes on Azeroth, the blast radius is contained. No Outland means no official competition for TBC-era servers, and nothing in the teaser threatens Era or Anniversary realms either.

The tracker side agrees something is close. Classic builds moved through the 1.60.0 chain into the 1.60.1 lineage while separate Camelot dev builds advanced in parallel — the exact shape you would expect days before an on-stage reveal. Track the builds on the [Classic+ page](/classic-plus/) and the full weekend schedule in the [BlizzCon preview](/news/blizzcon-2026-preview/).
