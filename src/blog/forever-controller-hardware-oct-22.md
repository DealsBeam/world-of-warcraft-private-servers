For twenty years the answer to playing World of Warcraft with a controller was a forum thread, a community addon, and a lot of patience. World of Warcraft: Forever ships first-party controller support, and we spent a day with the [official build](/news/forever-controller-support-hands-on/) and a second with [the mapping text](/blog/controller-mapping-forever-oct-21/) you need on your pad for it to work right. That changes the conversation from "can it be done" to "what should you hold", and what you hold is a genuine hardware decision.

## Why this guide is not just another controller list

A controller for World of Warcraft needs different things than a controller for a shooter. You are not compensating for aim assist. You are driving a bar of abilities, staging interrupts, and steering a character who sometimes needs to stop on a copper, and you are doing it all through a system Blizzard built so the names on your action bar stay readable on a television. The recommendations below are ordered by what goes wrong in that use, which is drift, latency and dead zones, in that order.

## The drift problem, and the pad that refuses to have it

Controller sticks wear. The symptom is your character creeping while you stand still, which on a private server with a raid behind you is the difference between standing in the fire and standing in the pull. The fix is Hall effect or TMR sticks, which use magnets instead of physical contact and cannot wear the same way. Discrete companies ship it before Microsoft does.

The <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D5CXKHKS?tag=indkin-20" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">GuliKit KK3 Pro</a> was about **$60** on 22 October and rated 4.4, with Hall effect sticks and triggers, and it is the pad we recommend if your existing controller has started to creep. There is a decent argument it is the one thing you should buy instead of a new television, because a drifting pad makes every game worse and a television only makes one game worse.

## The premium tier, and the honest case for it

The <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07SFKTLZM?tag=indkin-20" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">Xbox Elite Series 2</a> was about **$168** and rated 4.4 across 1,898 ratings, and the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FHHFBDNQ?tag=indkin-20" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener">GameSir G7 Pro</a> was about **$80** and rated 4.5. What the Elite buys is back paddles and swappable sticks, which in World of Warcraft means the interrupt nobody can reach goes on a paddle your finger is already on. The G7 Pro does most of that for half the price and is the better rated of the two.

## What to skip

**Anything that needs its own launcher to remap.** Your remapping tool should be as invisible as your game.

**The $17 generic wireless adapters.** The official pad connects over Bluetooth to a modern Windows machine. An extra dongle is another thing that drops.

**A pad for the raid, a mouse for the dungeon.** Blizzard's controller support is genuinely good for riding around and opening a map, and we say honestly in our [hands-on](/news/forever-controller-support-hands-on/) what it cannot do yet. Set your expectations before you spend.
