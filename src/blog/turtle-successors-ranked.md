When [Turtle WoW shut down on May 15](/blog/turtle-wow-shutdown-explained/) after Blizzard's consent judgment, roughly tens of thousands of Vanilla+ players went looking for a new home. Three months on, the succession is playing out exactly as the [shutdown-risk analysis](/news/private-server-shutdown-risk/) predicted: the codebase scattered, and five projects now claim its legacy.

Here's how they rank — by population evidence, not vibes.

## 1. Capybara WoW — the mass-market heir

**[Tracked: Capybara WoW](/servers/capybara-wow/) · Turtle 1.18.1 · ~10k online · large**

Run by former Turtle SEA admins on two realms, Capybara captured the single largest share of the exodus. It runs the actual Turtle 1.18.1 client — custom races, custom zones, the works — which made migration friction near-zero for refugees.

**Strengths:** Population begets population; the realm feels alive at every hour.
**Risks:** Largest target if the legal campaign resumes. Also the least "community-project" of the bunch — scale changes moderation.

## 2. Octo WoW — the structured revival

**[Tracked: Octo WoW](/servers/octowow/) · Turtle 1.18.1 · 3 realms · small**

Restored in July with three realms split by playstyle (N'Zoth PvE / C'Thun Hardcore / Y'Shaarj PvP) and a phased raid schedule through 2026-27. The HC realm is the differentiator — no other successor offers dedicated hardcore.

**Strengths:** Choice without fragmentation; roadmap discipline.
**Risks:** Small population spread across three realms can feel thin off-peak.

## 3. Ravencraft — the insider project

**[Tracked: Ravencraft](/servers/ravencraft/) · Turtle 1.18.1 · Medivh launched Aug 22 · small**

Built by former Turtle contributors, launched August 22 with a launch event running through August 29, and ships with [IchaLaunch](https://github.com/brutaliccus/IchaLaunch) — a proper launcher with addon and mod management. Credibility is its currency: these are the people who maintained Turtle.

**Strengths:** Institutional knowledge; tooling polish no competitor matches.
**Risks:** Single realm; needs sustained content cadence to hold the launch spike.

## 4. LunaticPTR — the cult classic

**[Tracked: LunaticPTR](/servers/lunaticptr/) · Turtle-derived challenge realm · tiny**

Zero XP, level-1 endgame, 10-man dungeon raids, restored Frostmane Hollow. It's not trying to be Turtle — it's trying to be the weirdest thing built on Turtle's bones, and it succeeds.

**Strengths:** Zero direct competition; passionate niche.
**Risks:** Ceiling is inherently low. A challenge realm is a side dish, never the main course.

## 5. Everwood WoW — the dark horse

**[Tracked: Everwood WoW](/servers/everwood-wow/) · Classless · unreleased · dev**

Formerly Caverns of Time, rebranded as Everwood with a confirmed classless system — putting it in Ascension-adjacent territory rather than pure Turtle lineage. No launch date.

**Strengths:** Classless Vanilla+ has proven demand (see: Ascension's 72k online).
**Risks:** Everything — no date, no population, competing against an established giant in its own niche.

## The wildcard: official Classic+

Every successor's real competitor isn't each other — it's [Project Camelot](/classic-plus/). If BlizzCon's September 12 reveal lands well with the Turtle demographic (custom content on Vanilla foundations), the successor wave faces the same squeeze Turtle did, minus the legal ambiguity. Our [predictions post](/news/blizzcon-predictions/) covers what to watch for.
