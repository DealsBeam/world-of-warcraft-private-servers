Official TBC Anniversary hit Phase 3 on August 27 with the same slice every TBC server knows: Hyjal Summit, Black Temple, Netherwing dailies, epic gems, and the next grind tier. On the Anniversary line the slice shipped as a clean progression window — attunements mattered, Hyjal trash gated harder than bosses, Netherwing became the post-raid loop, gems became the auction magnet. On private TBC the same slice has lived for a decade with different answers to the same gates. Here is how they line up, using our seven tracked TBC rows: [Moonwell](/servers/moonwell/), [Faebright](/servers/faebright/), [Whitemane — Sunwell](/servers/whitemane-sunwell/) (in dev), [Rising Gods — Back2Basics](/servers/rising-gods-back2basics/), [BloodWoW (TBC)](/servers/bloodwow-tbc/) and [Tirisgarde](/servers/tirisgarde/).

## Attunement — the gate most private realms skip

On Anniversary, Black Temple and Hyjal require the attunement chain: Shadow Labyrinth, Shattered Halls, Steamvaults and Arcatraz keys, then Vashj and Kael pre-quests, then the Hyjal introductory chain that hands you the vial quest, then Al'ar and Vashj kills that complete it. Guides for it — Black Temple attunement, Hyjal Summit attunement, Serpentshrine Cavern and The Eye — are being republished this week because the official line re-required them.

On private TBC, half our 5 playable rows do not require this chain at all. Instant-70 or high-rate realms skip it by design: you log, you buy the crafted set, you raid the first week. That is not a bug; it is the rate. On 1x or low-rate rows — Moonwell's veteran 2.4.3, Rising Gods' long-running German 2.4.3 — the chain exists and matters, and the official re-enable answers a question those realms have been answering quietly for years: how long should the chain take when the rest of the game around it is modern? Anniversary's answer is: about the same as 2007.

If you are new to private TBC and you want the original length, pick a 1x or low-rate row and do the chain. If you want the raid without the key, pick a high-rate row and treat attunement as lore you can read later. Both are on the tracker, both are tagged, both are honest.

## Hyjal — why the trash matters more than the bosses

Every 2.4.3 changelog that runs Hyjal has the same note: wave tuning, trash respawn, trash skip exploits. It is the only raid in the expansion where the corridor is the hard part. Anniversary shipped the waves at original size and did not add skips. Private realms that added skips or cut respawn are now visibly different from the official line.

For our tracked rows, Hyjal is also the raid most sensitive to population. Moonwell at 70-100 online runs Hyjal waves with the same mob count as Anniversary, but the competition for trash-tag and the window to recover after a wipe is smaller. Rising Gods at 70-100 German has to make the same waves work when the raid's language and time window are already narrow. That is a different design problem than the one Anniversary just solved, and it is why Hyjal guides that show the official pull order are only half the picture on private — the other half is how your realm handles the corridor when the group is five people short.

## Black Temple — what guides tell you and what they don't

Boss guides — Illidan, Council, Illidari, Teron, Gurtogg — translate directly between official and private. Suppression Room, Gorefiend ghost, Reliquary of Souls mechanic, Mother Shahraz prism, Council threat swap — all the same.

What does not translate is the tuning around them. Private 2.4.3 often runs with one or more of: slightly buffed trash health, slightly nerfed boss health, or a gear floor that Anniversary does not have (crafted sets at 2-3x, BoE world drops at inflated rates). The Black Temple guides being republished now show the official numbers. Use them as the reference, then ask your realm's note: what is different here? If the realm's post says "pre-nerf" or "post-nerf" or "custom scaling," that is the answer. Our rows that say "18-year German" or "x15 rates, BattleBots" are already telling you which side of that line they sit on.

## Netherwing — the daily loop that is a population engine

The other half of Phase 3 is not a raid at all. Netherwing dailies are the post-raid daily loop, and on Anniversary they shipped as the rep that everyone does because the ray is the rare mount. On private TBC with smaller populations the same dailies often become the whole game, not the side game. Moonwell at 70-100 has to make egg competition work when the zone has five people, not fifty. Faebright at 0-50 and Tirisgarde at a handful have to make the chain worth doing when the daily group is two people and a bot.

Netherwing guides — the egg spots, the mine, the race, the revered grind — are useful on private exactly because the spawns are the same. What changes is whether the grind is social. On a small private realm, Netherwing is where you meet the other ten people who still need the rep. That is not in the guide, but it is why private TBC keeps the chain even when the ray is not rare.

## Where the tracker sits

Seven TBC rows. Five playable (Moonwell small, Rising Gods small, Faebright tiny, BloodWoW tiny, Tirisgarde tiny), one in development (Whitemane Sunwell, the TBC leg of the Whitemane network, promising a fresh 2.4.3 realm), one dead (Felmyst, offline since 2023). None are large. TBC as a lived game on the tracker is small, specific, and mostly 2.4.3 1x or low-rate with a long history — not the Anniversary's medium-population corridor.

None of that changes because official Phase 3 shipped. But the official phase does answer the question the tracker hears most about TBC private servers — "is this just doing what official already does?" — with: for this patch window, yes, the same two raids for the same two reasons. The difference is the track underneath. Private TBC has had to solve the small-population version of the same content. Watching how official tuned it for a medium population answers whether private's answers were too generous, too stingy, or just right.

For now, official is the reference, private is the variation. Use the attunement and Netherwing guides as the reference text; use your realm's rate and population as the translation.
