Blizzard's September 23 Legacy article replaces much of the speculation that shaped our first guide. The system is account-wide, but alternate characters are optional. The article confirms 65 Challenges, 21 named perks across three trees, and a 16-point spending cap per character at launch.

## Correction history

- **September 13 pre-release read, corrected September 24:** ~~17 perks captured~~. Blizzard's official article names **21 perks**, seven in each tree.
- **September 13 pre-release read, updated September 24:** ~~Talented starts at level 9~~. Blizzard says Talented unlocks talent points up to five levels earlier than normal. The beta rank curve reads levels 9, 8, 7, 6, and 5, so the old value described only one pre-release rank.
- **September 13 pre-release read, updated September 24:** ~~Unlocks and higher ranks need verification~~. The three trees, point economy, access rule, perk names, and launch rewards are now official. Numeric rank values remain beta reads and can still change.

The fixed level 60 cap is not the thing being corrected here. The change is to Legacy's structure and the evidence behind it.

## TL;DR

- **65 Legacy Challenges** are divided across classes, tradeskills, PvP, adventure, dungeons, and raids.
- **16 Legacy Points per character** can be spent at launch across Professions, Adventure, and Resourcefulness.
- **One character can earn up to 29 points**, so alts are optional even though the points are account-wide.
- **21 named perks** are split evenly across the three trees.
- Legacy access normally begins with the first point at **level 25**, although skill 150 or full world-map exploration can also unlock it.
- Four launch rewards are confirmed: a Replica Ironforge Air Rifle, Spectral Bear Cub, Spectral Bear Tabard, and Reins of the Spectral Bear.

## How Legacy works

Legacy has three connected parts:

1. **Legacy Challenges** award one account-wide point each for first-time character progression.
2. **Legacy Perks** let each character spend points independently in one of three trees.
3. **Legacy Rewards** track total account points and unlock toys, a pet, a tabard, and a mount.

The important distinction is between earning and spending. Points belong to the account, but each character gets its own 16-point budget at launch. A player can put points into a leveler on one character and a raid character on another, or fit the entire launch budget onto one character.

## The 65 Challenges

| Category | Launch points | Official examples |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Classes | 27 | Each class at levels 25, 45, and 60 |
| Tradeskills | 18 | Each non-gathering primary profession at skill 150, 225, and 300 |
| PvP | 12 | Ranks 3, 7, 10, 13, and 14; four Battleground Exalted ranks; three Field of Honor journey weeks |
| Adventure | 2 | Explore the full world map; complete the Lord Valthalak dungeon questline |
| Dungeons | 3 | Final boss in each level 15-25, 26-45, and 46-60 bracket |
| Raids | 3 | Onyxia, all 13 Hyjal Summit bosses, and all 8 Barrow Deeps bosses |

The categories total 65. A single character can earn 29 points from its class, tradeskill, PvP, adventure, dungeon, and raid milestones without creating a second character.

## The 21 perks

### Professions

- **Working Overtime** increases the chance to gain a skill point, including in class skills such as Lockpicking.
- **Bountiful Harvest** adds chances to find Scarce resources while mining, herbing, or skinning.
- **Bartering** reduces vendor prices.
- **Performance Bonus** can grant extra Merchant's Favor from the Azeroth Commerce Authority or Durotar Supply and Logistics.
- **Master Chef** gives cooking a chance to produce an extra result.
- **Luremaster** gives fishing a chance to catch extra fish.
- **Dedicated Study** raises the lowest tradeskill on a daily cooldown, or grants Elemental Essences after all relevant skills reach 300.

### Adventure

- **Well Rested** increases the rested experience cap and accumulation speed.
- **Thrill of Adventure** restores a small amount of health and mana after non-trivial kills.
- **High Alert** improves stealth detection outside battlegrounds.
- **Field Guide** reduces the cooldown for placing camping features.
- **Field Medicine** shortens Recently Bandaged, except in dungeons, raids, and battlegrounds.
- **Talented** unlocks talent points up to five levels earlier than normal.
- **Frequent Flier** lowers flight costs and increases flight speed.

### Resourcefulness

- **Gourmand** extends food buff duration.
- **The Quick and the Dead** speeds movement while dead and makes party and raid buffs free after resurrection.
- **Reinforce** reduces durability loss from death.
- **For Great Honor** increases Honor gains from PvP.
- **Permanence** extends party, raid, and camping buffs.
- **Diplomat** increases reputation gains.
- **Reagent Economy** removes purchasable reagent requirements from class abilities and makes Tier 1 camping features free to craft.

Blizzard confirms the names and broad effects. Exact rank values in the [Forever hub](/classic-plus/#systems) remain beta-client reads unless the official article gives a launch value.

## Rewards and Hardcore

The four launch Legacy Rewards are account-wide cosmetics and toys. Existing beta captures place them at 15, 25, 40, and 55 total points, but Blizzard's September 23 article confirms the rewards without publishing those thresholds. Keep the thresholds marked as beta captures until the UI verifies them.

Hardcore is not a free shortcut into the normal account-wide pool. Non-Hardcore Challenges do not appear in Hardcore, while a Hardcore Challenge can grant its challenge in other rulesets. PvP Challenges cannot be completed in Hardcore, and Blizzard plans 12 Hardcore-specific Challenges when that ruleset launches.

## Camping remains the social half

Legacy is the account progression layer. Camping is the outdoor social layer: players place campfires, contribute crafted objects through professions, and share campsite buffs. Field Guide, Permanence, Reagent Economy, Field Medicine, and related perks interact with that system, but Blizzard's new Legacy article focuses on the account-wide Challenge, Perk, and Reward structure rather than adding a new Camping ruleset. For a current beta route and experience-value reference, see the [Cozy Sleeping Bag guide](/blog/forever-cozy-sleeping-bag-route/).

## What this changes for players

The old 17-perk read was useful as a beta snapshot, but it understated the official system. The corrected picture is more flexible and more legible:

- Alts are an option, not a requirement.
- Spending is per character, while Challenges and Rewards are account-wide.
- The launch cap is 16 points per character, with 65 Challenges feeding the larger account track.
- Future content can add Challenges, Perks, and Rewards without changing the basic account-wide premise.

For private-server players, the useful comparison is structural rather than visual: Forever is adding account-wide convenience without turning every alt into a fresh subscription-sized project. The open question is how much of the 65-point track remains relevant after launch, especially when new content adds more Challenges and rewards.
