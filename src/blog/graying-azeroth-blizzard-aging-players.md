Blizzard's players grew up. They didn't leave — they aged in place. That fact shapes everything from Classic+ to private servers.

## TL;DR

- Legacy Blizzard skews 28-50+. Only Overwatch (15-28) and Hearthstone (20-35) reach young.
- Adults spend more per head. Fewer players, higher ARPU — the economic shield.
- Microsoft covers youth via Minecraft/Game Pass. Blizzard keeps the high-value adults.
- Estimates below are direction, not data. No sourced age survey exists here.

## The split (estimates, flagged)

- WoW Retail + Classic: ~28-45, peak 25-34. Classic monetizes 90s/00s nostalgia directly.
- StarCraft I/II: ~30-50+. 1998 heritage, analytical niche.
- Diablo series: ~25-45, core ~35. Purchasing power bracket.
- Warcraft RTS: ~35-55+. Retro + strategy veterans.
- Hearthstone: ~20-35. Mobile bridges down.
- Overwatch 2: ~15-28. The only youth anchor. Free-to-play does the work.

## Why it matters here

Thirty-somethings with jobs, kids, tight leisure and fat wallets are exactly who wants a "forever home" Classic+ and exactly who plays private servers: fixed costs, no grind disrespect, nostalgia on demand. The demographic cliff IS the Classic+ business case — and the emu scene's customer base. Blizzard fights private servers while selling to the same aging cohort that fills them.

## The Microsoft math

Minecraft + Game Pass own childhood. Blizzard owns high-margin adult PC. Overwatch/mobile trickle youth upward. Portfolio covers the lifecycle; no single title must do it all.

## What's next

Watch whether BlizzCon pitches Classic+ at permanence (forever-home language) or seasons. Permanence talk confirms they read the graying correctly.
