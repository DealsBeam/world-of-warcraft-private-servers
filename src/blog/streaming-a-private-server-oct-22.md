The private server scene has always had its own streamers. Watch any realm launch and somebody is already live on it, and the reason the scene is bigger than its player numbers suggests is partly that it produces hours of free content. If you want to do that, you do not need a studio. You need three things, and only one of them is about the game.

## The rule that decides your shopping list

**Do you need a capture card at all?** If the game runs on the same machine you stream from, yes. If you run the game on a second PC or a spare machine, you do not, because the streaming software captures the game window directly. A capture card exists to pull the picture out of a second device, usually a console or a second PC, through HDMI.

That single question decides whether the biggest line item in this guide is something you need or something you were about to buy for the wrong reason.

## Two rules that are not optional

**Ask the operator before you stream a private server.** Not because they own the raid footage, but because a stream is promotion. Every realm operator we track would rather know, and the ones who are serious will give you their launch date, their rules and their Discord, which makes the stream better. It is also the difference between a stream people watch and a stream that gets a realm a legal problem.

**Keep your account details off screen.** Passwords, character names of other players, server addresses, and Discord invite links are all things you should not put on stream, and the last one in particular gets your account stolen in a week. Blizzard's terms also do not cover the music you play over a stream, so use the game's own score and the sound effects, which are the better soundtrack anyway.
