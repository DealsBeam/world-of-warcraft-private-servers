Private servers let you play World of Warcraft outside Blizzard's official servers — classic expansions, custom content, higher rates, or communities Blizzard doesn't serve. The process is simple once you know the pieces. Here's the whole thing in one page.

## The three things you need

1. **A client.** This is the game itself — the files that render Azeroth. Each server runs a specific expansion (Vanilla 1.12, WotLK 3.3.5a, Cataclysm, etc.), and you need the matching client version. Most servers link their recommended client on their website or Discord.
2. **A server address.** Private servers are identified by a hostname like `play.greyhaven.com`. You put this in a file called `realmlist.wtf`.
3. **An account.** Sign up on the server's website. Nothing more exotic than that.

## Setting the realmlist

In the client folder there's a file called `realmlist.wtf`. It contains one line, `set realmlist <hostname>`. Replace the hostname with your chosen server's address, save, and launch. Some servers also provide a tiny launcher that does this for you.

The exact filename and format vary slightly by expansion, but it's always a plain text file in the client root. If you're stuck, the server's setup guide covers it — every serious realm has one.

## Picking a server that won't vanish

This is the part nobody tells you upfront. Private servers die, and your character dies with them. Things that keep a server alive:

- **A large, active population.** Empty servers close.
- **A long track record.** Realms that have survived for years (like [Warmane](/servers/warmane/)) are safer than shiny new ones.
- **An active development team.** Scripting quality and content releases depend on them.
- **Transparent funding.** Donations have to actually pay the bills, not line pockets.

Our [server tracker](/) tracks current status — playable, in development, or closed — for 50+ realms, updated as the scene changes. It's the fastest way to see what's actually running right now.

## What's risky

Private servers are unlicensed third-party projects. Blizzard has sent cease-and-desist letters and filed lawsuits against operators for years — most recently [Turtle WoW and Stormforge shut down in May 2026](/blog/turtle-wow-shutdown-explained/), and [Ascension is fighting a RICO lawsuit](/news/blizzard-rico-ascension/). If Blizzard targets a server, it closes, and that's usually the end of it.

On the client side, play smart:

- **Only download clients from the server's own site or Discord** — repacked downloads are a classic malware vector.
- **Don't reuse your retail passwords** on private-server sites.
- **Expect no privacy guarantees.** Server operators can see your data, and some have been subpoenaed in legal cases.

## Starting tips

- **Pick your expansion first, then your server.** WotLK is the most populated corner of the scene — see [our WotLK roundup](/blog/best-wotlk-private-servers-2026/) — but Vanilla, Vanilla+, MoP, and custom realms all have strong communities.
- **Join the Discord before committing.** It tells you more about a server's health than any landing page.
- **Start with a 1x or low-rate realm to learn; use funservers when you already know the game.**
- **Modern clients exist for old expansions.** If you're playing Vanilla, the [OctoWoW client](/guides/octowow/) brings a modern 1.12 client with addon support to the scene.

Private servers are a rabbit hole with a real community at the bottom. Start with one realm, give it a week, and you'll know whether the scene is for you.
