Our tracker rates a project's population only when the project publishes something we can read, or when we can count it ourselves. Everything else is **unknown**, and unknown is deliberately not a polite word for dead. This is the state of that data as of October 17 2026, across 236 tracked servers.

## The headline numbers

- **236 servers tracked.** 186 rated playable, 37 in development, 13 dead.
- **111 of the 223 live entries carry a population band.** 112 do not.
- Bands in use: **11 large, 8 medium, 31 small, 62 tiny, 124 unknown** across the full set.

Those 13 dead entries are one of the reasons the unknown count is as high as it is: several sit in the tracker as shutdown records rather than as running servers, and we do not invent a band for a realm nobody can log in to.

Two things stand out. First, **Wrath is both the largest era by count and one of the least transparent**: 115 entries, half of them publish nothing. Second, **Legion is the opposite**, with only one silent entry out of eight. That is not because Legion operators are more honest. It is because the modern Legion scene is dominated by a handful of projects that run public counters, and because that scene is small enough that we could check most of them.

## Why so many fields are empty

The silence has a consistent shape, and it is rarely sinister:

- **A counter is a claim, not a census.** Where a project publishes one, we record it and label it self-reported. WoW Circle's front page read **22,558 total online** on October 3, and Sirus's own counter read **18,468**. Neither is an audited figure, and both move with time of day.
- **Some large projects simply have no counter.** Warmane is one of the biggest names in the scene and its news block is a stale archive, which tells you nothing about liveness. Our own September correction on that entry is the reason we put more weight on what a server does than on what its homepage says.
- **A maintenance page is not a death.** Dalaran-WoW currently returns an HTTP 503 maintenance notice. An operator configured that message, which is different from an abandoned host, so the entry stays live with an unknown band.
- **New and developing projects have nothing to count yet.** A realm in early access with a level cap has a number, but it is not a population figure worth putting beside a long-running server.

## The concentration worth knowing about

The eleven large entries are the top of the pyramid, and they are not evenly distributed:

- The **Whitemane network alone accounts for five** of the eleven, across its Cataclysm and Wrath realms.
- The list also includes **Warmane, Firestorm, Sirus, WoW Circle and uWoW**, which between them cover WotLK, multiple eras, and a Legion realm.

Below that, the tracker is a long tail: **62 tiny entries against 31 small and 8 medium**. If you are picking a server for the long haul, the practical advice is not "pick the biggest". It is "pick one with a nonzero number you can see, in the era you want", then check it again a month later.

## What an unknown band means for you

1. **Unknown is not empty.** Several unknown entries are perfectly playable projects that keep their numbers private, or that use a launcher where the count is not public.
2. **Tiny is not a red flag, but it is a decision.** A 19-player twink realm and a 3-player hardcore realm are different risks, and both are rated tiny honestly.
3. **Recheck before you invest a character.** A band is a snapshot with a date on it, which is more than most lists give you.
4. **If a project adds a counter, tell us.** We will read it, date it, and update the entry, with a correction noted where we had it wrong.
