Blizzard's Legacy System is the first account-wide progression system in a game that otherwise refuses to be account-wide, and it has one number in it that changes how you should play: **you can spend at most 16 Legacy Points on any single character at launch.**

Everything else in this system is a list. That cap is the design.

## What you are collecting

Access arrives with your **first Legacy Point**, which most players earn when their first character hits **level 25**. You can also get in early by reaching **150** in a non-gathering primary profession, or by exploring the entire world map. Once unlocked, the system opens from a shield icon in the menu or the hotkey **Y**.

There are **65 Legacy Challenges** in six categories. Each one you complete is worth **one point**, and points are **Battle.net account-wide** and usable by every non-Hardcore character on the account.

| Category | Points | The specific gates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classes | 27 | Every class to level 25, then 45, then 60 |
| Tradeskills | 18 | Every non-gathering primary to 150, 225, 300 |
| PvP | 12 | Ranks 3, 7, 10, 13, 14 (5), Exalted with each Battleground rep (4), weeks 4, 7 and 10 of the Field of Honor season (3) |
| Adventure | 2 | Full world map, plus the Lord Valthalak dungeon set questline |
| Dungeons | 3 | Final boss in every dungeon at 15-25, 26-45, 46-60 |
| Raids | 3 | Onyxia, all 13 Hyjal bosses, all 8 Barrow Deeps bosses |

Do the arithmetic on that first row and it tells you the shape of the system. **27 points requires levelling nine separate classes three times over.** Classes is the biggest earner and it is also the one that demands alts, in a system whose designer says you do not need them.

## Why 16 is the number that matters

Blizzard explains the cap twice, and both reasons are worth reading because they point in opposite directions:

> "First, new players will not be so far behind established players that they can't catch up. Second, 16 points can be reached on a single character, so it is not required to create alts."

The second claim is checkable against their own table. One character can earn **3 from levelling, up to 6 from tradeskills, 12 from PvP, 2 from Adventure, and 6 from dungeons and raids, for up to 29 points total**. Against a spend cap of 16.

So a solo player really can reach the cap without an alt, and really can out-earn it by 13 points. Which means **the surplus is wasted on that character**, and the design decision is that a hard cap beats an unbounded currency for keeping new players in the same race.

That is a defensible choice and we are not going to call it bad. It is also the reason a veteran account with nine level-45 characters has banked 27 points and can only spend 16 on any one of them, which is a strange feeling to hand someone three weeks before launch.

## What the points buy

Three trees, **Professions**, **Adventure** and **Resourcefulness**, and each character spends its points independently, so you can build one character for gathering and another for raids.

The ones we would plan around:

- **Working Overtime**, chance to gain a skill point in **all** tradeskills including Lockpicking.
- **Bountiful Harvest**, more Scarce resources from mining, herbing and skinning.
- **Bartering**, lower vendor prices.
- **Dedicated Study**, a daily cooldown for faster tradeskill gains, or free Elemental Essences once everything is 300.
- **Well Rested**, raises the rested XP cap and accumulates faster. This is the one to buy first on a fresh character and it is not close.
- **Field Guide**, cuts the **1-hour** cooldown on camping features.
- **Talented**, unlocks talent points up to **5 levels lower** than normal.
- **Frequent Flier**, cheaper and faster flights.
- **The Quick and the Dead**, dead players move faster running back to their body, which is a quality-of-life perk that quietly deletes a long grind.

Two new tradeskill factions are named as sources of activity: the **Azeroth Commerce Authority** and **Durotar Supply and Logistics**.

## The part that is not explained

**Legacy Rewards is named as the third component and then never described** in the 23 September post. Two of three are covered in detail, and the third is not, so we do not know what it is.

That gap matters more than a missing paragraph, because if Legacy Rewards turns out to be an account-wide reward rather than a per-character perk, then the 16-point cap is a much smaller deal than it currently looks. It might be a catch, a title, a mount, or a currency. We are not going to guess, and we would not buy a guide that did.

## What we cannot tell you

Anything in game. We have no beta client, this is a design post read on 2 October, and the cap in particular is the kind of number that moves during a beta. Both published posts elsewhere in this coverage carry Blizzard's own warning that systems "may continue to evolve".

What we can tell you is that if you are planning to level one character seriously and treat alts as optional, **buy Well Rested first and spend the rest as you unlock it**, because the cap means unspent points are not a resource and a single character can earn more than it can spend.
