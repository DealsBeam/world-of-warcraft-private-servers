A player who has levelled a Classic character has a strong prior for what a talent tree is. Forever keeps most of that prior intact, which is precisely why the changes are easy to miss.

**This is a reading guide, not a build guide.** Four of ten classes have published deep dives, so anything written here about a specific spec is provisional. We have written it that way deliberately.

## What has not changed

The parts Blizzard has confirmed as unchanged are the parts that define the familiar experience:

- **Talents unlock at level 10**, same as original WoW.
- **The number of rows is the same.**
- **One-point milestone talents at 11, 21 and 31 points**, same as original WoW.
- **Dual Specialization at level 40**, same as original WoW.
- **Talents are changed at a class trainer in a major city.**

If your memory says "row 5 is where I commit at 31 points", that memory still works.

## What has changed

**A new 16-point milestone.** Both deep dives say one-point milestones now sit at 11, 21 and 31, "now joined by a new 16-point milestone". That is a fourth milestone row on the classes it has been documented for.

**Several talents are now baseline.** Blessing of Kings and Improved Mark of the Wild, which were talents, are baseline for the classes that had them. That is not a small change: a talent tree that keeps its shape while some of what used to be in it becomes free changes what the remaining points are worth.

**And more, per class.** Hunter gets **Aimed Shot at level 20** and traps usable in combat, plus **Foxes as a new pet family**. Priest gets **Fear Ward and Devouring Plague at 20 regardless of race**, **Divine Spirit at 30**, and **Shadow Word: Death as a baseline ability at level 32**.

## The trap, stated plainly

The tree looks the same, so it reads the same, so a returning player budgets their points against Classic assumptions and is wrong.

The specific way this bites: **a baseline spell is worth more than a talent you chose**, because it does not compete with anything else for a point. Blessing of Kings as a talent is a decision with an opportunity cost. Blessing of Kings as baseline is free. A player comparing their Forever tree to their Classic tree point for point is comparing a tree with fewer real choices in it, and the shape is identical, so nothing looks different until you try to build.

The second bite is the 16-point milestone changing what "31 points" means as an endpoint. It does not remove the old milestone; it inserts a decision before the one you had.

## What we cannot tell you, and this is the important half

**Six classes have no published deep dive**: Paladin, Rogue, Shaman, Mage, Death Knight, Monk.

The 16-point milestone is currently specified for **four of ten classes**. If it is universal, every class in the game gains a fourth milestone row. If it is not, then the classes nobody has documented play on the original three-milestone structure, and the game has two different talent shapes in it.

We are not going to guess which, and neither should a guide.

Both posts also carry the same warning: *"class abilities and talents may continue to evolve throughout the beta test and are subject to additional changes."* These are design notes read on 2 October, **33 days before launch**, from a game none of us has played.

## What a returning player should do on launch day

Read the class tooltip before spending a point, not after. The baseline list is the thing that has moved and it is the thing that is easiest to miss, because nothing about the tree's appearance has changed.

If you levelled a character to 60 in Classic, the fastest way to get this wrong is to spend your first 31 points the way you did in 2006. The tree will let you. It just will not be the tree you remember.
