We published about **thirty World of Warcraft: Forever items** in four days. Then we read one of Blizzard's own news posts and found **three explainers we had never written up**.

This is a cheap technique and we should have been running it all along, so here it is written down.

## The method

Take Blizzard's **Wow Weekly**, which is a link digest, and compare its links against our own published archive. That is the whole procedure. It takes about ten minutes and it finds gaps that no amount of our own research would, because we were researching bottom-up and the index is top-down.

**The 25 September Wow Weekly** linked six Forever explainers:

| Blizzard's link | Our coverage, before 2 Oct |
| --- | --- |
| Podcast episode 1 | covered |
| Choose your ruleset | **gap** |
| Create a name of your own | partial |
| Create the hero you want to be | **gap** |
| The new Skyborne race | covered |
| The Legacy System | **gap** |

Three gaps out of six, in a week we considered ourselves prolific on this subject.

## Why we had the gaps

**We were reading datamines instead of explainers.** Our beta coverage had been mounts, quest text, addon tooling, client workarounds, build numbers. All of it useful and all of it bottom-up. The explainers contain the things a player asks about first: what is a ruleset, how do racials work, what is this account-wide system.

**Nothing in our process asked what Blizzard considered essential.** We had no habit of reading their index. The index is the publisher telling you, in priority order, what they think a new player needs to understand, and we were ignoring it because it was not a source we had previously used.

## What closed the gaps

The Legacy System gap closed first, because it turned out to be the densest document Blizzard has published about this branch: **65 challenges, six categories, and a 16-point cap per character**. That became [our guide](/blog/forever-legacy-system-horizontal-progression-guide/).

The naming gap produced the single most consequential sentence we have published, and it came from an explainer we had only partially covered. Blizzard describes character names as gaining "more room to breathe through a new **two-name structure** designed for a larger, **realmless** Azeroth."

**Realmless.** That one word answers the sharding question that our own coverage had been circling for weeks, and it appears in a post about naming.

## The technique, restated

**Before you research a subject, read the publisher's index.** Not the index of what you find interesting, the index of what they think matters.

For a launch this is doubly useful, because the index is where the gaps between "documented" and "published" show up. Blizzard has deep-dived **four of ten classes**. Six have nothing. That is visible in the index in a way that is not visible from any single post.

It is also how you catch a stale source. All three fan references we track sit on a build from 25 September, and we only know that because we compare what they publish against the manifest.

## What we are changing

We are reading the index weekly, and treating "an explainer we have not covered" as a queue item rather than as something we get to when there is time. Thirty items in four days is not coverage if three of the six things the publisher considers essential are missing from it.
