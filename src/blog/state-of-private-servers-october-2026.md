The tracker stands at **236 servers** in mid-October 2026: **186 playable, 37 in development, and 13 dead**. Here is what those numbers say on their own, with no outside reporting and no forecasts, exactly as we did in [the August snapshot](/blog/tracker-snapshot-august-2026/).

One honesty note up front: our count more than doubled since August, from 104 entries to 236, and almost all of that is **our own coverage growing**, not the scene doubling. We keep scanning directories and probing named projects, so the totals are a measure of how much we have looked at, not of how many realms opened. Comparisons below are about proportions, not raw growth.

## Wrath is still half the scene

- **WotLK:** 117 entries, just under **50 percent** of everything tracked.
- **Vanilla+:** 25.
- **Vanilla:** 23.
- **TBC:** 15.
- **Cataclysm:** 12.
- **Multi-expansion networks:** 10.
- **Legion:** 9.
- **Mists of Pandaria:** 8.
- **Classless:** 6.
- **WoD, Dragonflight-era and other pockets:** a handful between them.

The middle expansions are where the scene is thin. Cataclysm manages 12 entries, and it is carried by a single network that we will get to. Legion's 9 and MoP's 8 look small until you remember that both are counted mostly through multi-realm networks rather than standalone projects.

## One network holds the top of the table

Of the **11 servers we rate large, five share the Whitemane name**: [Gilneas](/servers/whitemane-gilneas/) and [Maelstrom](/servers/whitemane-maelstrom/) on Cataclysm, [Frostmourne](/servers/whitemane-frostmourne/) on WotLK, [Stormstout](/servers/whitemane-stormstout/) on MoP, and [Sunwell](/servers/whitemane-sunwell/) in development on TBC as the network's fifth era.

Put plainly: **nearly half of the largest realms we track are one operator across four expansions.** Add [Warmane](/servers/warmane/) and [Chromiecraft](/servers/chromiecraft/) on WotLK, [Sirus](/servers/sirus/) for custom Wrath, [WoW Circle](/servers/wow-circle/) and [uWoW](/servers/uwow/) on later expansions, and [Firestorm](/servers/firestorm/) on the newest client, and the entire large tier is a short list of established networks.

This is the structural story of 2026. The private scene did not get more large servers this year. It got **more small ones**, and the large ones consolidated into networks that run several eras under one account.

## More than half the scene has no readable population

The population bands, as of this count:

- **Unknown: 124** (52.5 percent).
- **Tiny: 62.**
- **Small: 31.**
- **Large: 11.**
- **Medium: 8.**

Two readings matter here.

First, **the honest one: we do not know how big most of this scene is.** More than half of the tracker carries no population band, because the project publishes no counter we can read. That is not a gap in the tracker so much as a property of the scene: server operators routinely publish a Discord member count instead of a player count, and those are different numbers. We explained the reasoning at length in [the census post](/blog/tracker-census-185-entries-oct-5/).

Second, **the medium tier is still nearly empty: 8 servers.** A realm in the 500 to 2,000 concurrent range tends to either grow into a network or shrink into the tiny column. The middle is where stability is hardest to buy.

## The dead column is all C&D now

Thirteen entries are dead, and the pattern is stark:

- **2022:** [ArgusWoW](/servers/arguswow/) (cease and desist).
- **2023:** [Felmyst](/servers/felmyst/) (C&D), [WORS](/servers/wors-world-of-runescape/) and [Everlook](/servers/everlook/) (both abandoned).
- **May 2026:** [Turtle WoW](/servers/turtle-wow/), [Stormforge Mistblade](/servers/stormforge-mistblade/), [Stormforge Wrath](/servers/stormforge-wrath/), all C&D.
- **September 4 2026:** the five Ascension-network realms, [Ascension](/servers/ascension/), [Bronzebeard](/servers/bronzebeard/), [Conquest of Azeroth](/servers/conquest-of-azeroth/), [Area 52](/servers/area-52/) and [Elune](/servers/elune/), all C&D on one day.
- **September 15 2026:** [Project Alterac](/servers/project-alterac/) (abandoned).

Nine of the thirteen are C&D, and **eight of those nine arrived in 2026**. Abandonment used to be the common death. This year, the common death is a legal letter. The full writeup is in [the 2026 shutdown roundup](/blog/private-servers-shut-down-2026/) and [the crackdown explainer](/blog/blizzard-2026-private-server-crackdown/).

## October has been a launch month

The gates we tracked and verified this month, in order:

- **October 15:** [Sulvus Reforged](/servers/sulvus-reforged/), WotLK 3.3.5a PvP.
- **October 16:** uWoW's **Mists of Pandaria x10** realm, which its own statistics page later showed online with 492 players, and Nozdor's **DUNE**, a fresh WotLK x1 to x5 realm whose page now reads that Dune is open.
- **October 16:** [lordaeron.eu](/servers/lordaeron/)'s **Rimeheart**, a custom progressive Wrath realm, confirmed online in its own status feed.
- **October 17:** WoW Circle's fresh **Legion x5** realm, dated by its own news to 19:00 Moscow.

That is four projects opening realms inside a week in October, and it does not count the six on [the launch calendar](/blog/launch-calendar/) still to come, from Anachronos on October 22 to the Shadowsong and Luminary dates at the end of the month.

## The Forever horizon

The single biggest variable in the next month is not a private server at all. Blizzard's **World of Warcraft: Forever** launches on **November 4 2026**, with the beta's last full day on October 21, name reservation from October 27, and the first raids dated to December 9. Our [launch calendar](/blog/launch-calendar/) carries the verified dates and the announced ones separately.

The private scene has already answered the question this raises, in the shape of its own numbers. It has not emptied out ahead of Forever, and the October launch cluster is evidence of the opposite: projects are opening realms into the same window. Whether that holds after November 4 is [the real question for the private server scene](/blog/forever-private-servers-day-after-launch/), and it is the one we will be counting next.

## What the numbers say

Read together: a scene that is **broad and shallow**, half of it Wrath, more than half of it unmeasured, most of its large realms owned by a handful of networks, and its death column now dominated by cease-and-desist. It is not shrinking. It is consolidating, while a wave of small projects keeps opening into a month that ends with Blizzard launching the thing they all emulate. The next count will say whether that wave survives the fourth of November.
