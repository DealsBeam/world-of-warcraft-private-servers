Moonwell has been running Burning Crusade since **January 2011**, which makes it one of the oldest continuously operating realms in this scene, and this morning it read **854 players online**. That is the largest confirmed TBC population on our tracker.

The complication is that Moonwell runs **two realms at radically different sizes**, and it is easy to walk into the wrong one. This morning:

- **Moonwell x5: 854 online**
- **Moonwell x100: 5 online**

Same project, same company, a hundredfold difference. Our own entry had this backwards and rated the whole thing a tier too low, which is corrected in [today's sweep post](/news/stale-sweep-batch-2-corrections-sep-29/) with the old value struck through.

The operator is **Torg Company**, registered in Bishkek, Kyrgyz Republic. The site is in Russian, the server is European, and the client is your own 2.4.3.

## What "x5" and "x100" actually mean

The multiplier is experience rate, and on Moonwell the **x5 realm is the mature one** while x100 is the speedrun side.

The site's own account-exchange page says it accepts transfers **from Warmane, Chromie, Project Epoch and Project Ascension**, with special conditions for guilds. Two of those four projects are gone or in upheaval, and that is worth noting for a different reason: Moonwell has been the landing spot for people displaced from elsewhere in this scene, and it is one of the few long-running realms still large enough to absorb them.

## Content is phased, and it is past Phase Five

Moonwell does not open all of Outland at once. Content arrives in dated phases, and the site publishes each one:

- **Phase One** opened December 30, 2025, with Karazhan, Magtheridon's Lair and Gruul's Lair.
- **Phase Two** opened February 6, 2026, adding Tempest Keep and Serpentshrine Cavern in pre-nerf mode, and converting accumulated arena points to honor at 10:1.
- **Phase Three** opened March 7, 2026, adding Black Temple and Hyjal Summit in post-nerf mode, with **Illidan, the Council and Archimonde on increased health** as the stated exceptions.
- **Phase Four** opened May 13, 2026, adding **Zul'Aman as a 10-player raid** with standard boss values, plus Hyjal and Black Temple attunement changes.
- **Phase Five** opened July 22, 2026, opening the Isle of Quel'Danas and the Shattered Sun Offensive campaign.

The pre-nerf and post-nerf distinction is the detail that matters if you care about encountering an era as intended. Phase Two opened Serpentshrine and Tempest Keep at pre-nerf health, and Phase Three deliberately opened Illidan nerfed and then put the health back on three specific bosses. A realm that chooses which version of a fight you meet is making a statement.

## A new core in testing, announced August 25

The most recent development note, dated **25 August 2026**, describes months of work on a replacement core, running in parallel with the live x5 realm on a **separate test server**. The project's description is that it consolidated work and fixes from other cores into "a completely different level of performance that the server has never seen before."

That is an unusual thing to do to a realm of this size and age, and it carries a risk worth naming: a fifteen-year-old character base on a new core is where regressions live. The fact that they are testing on a separate realm rather than swapping the live one is the right call and the reason to watch it rather than worry about it.

## Fast Start, and the reasoning behind it

There are two boost products, and the justification given is the most honest line on the site:

> "The Burning Crusade audience today mostly consists of adults: they have jobs, families, their own responsibilities, and no longer have a student's amount of free time. Many tell us themselves that they want to play, but aren't ready to start with..."

**Fast Start** gives a level-70 character with your race, class and appearance of choice, flight paths unlocked, spells learned, starter gear, professions at 375, a ground mount, four 8-slot bags and armour and weapons. **Fast Start++** skips to the final content phase directly, which is the same bet TBC5Legends makes with instant 70.

The ageing-player argument is the right read of this scene and it is the same argument that runs through [IceDNicco's heirloom gift](/blog/therawow-solo-wotlk-guide/) and ArdenWoW's no-death challenge. Servers that keep pretending the audience is twenty are the ones that end up empty.

## The records

| Date | Moonwell x5 online |
| --- | --- |
| Feb 2, 2026 | 1,656 |
| Apr 27, 2026 | 2,347 |
| Jul 23, 2026 | **2,987** (all-time high) |
| Sep 29, 2026 | 854 |

The current figure is well below the July peak, which is worth stating plainly rather than burying: **the realm is well below its own high and is not near 3,000 today.** 854 is a healthy number and it is roughly a quarter of what this project has shown it can do. If a month from now it is back near 2,500, the July figure was a spike; if it is still in the hundreds, something changed.

There is also a **leveling challenge** on x1 rates with a unique reward for reaching 70: a Fantastic Turtle mount, a Hard Road Seeker tabard, and a 36-slot Foror's Crate.

## What we cannot tell you

Whether the new core is an improvement in practice, because the note is promotional and the test realm is separate. Whether the x100 realm is worth playing at five concurrent, which on the evidence is not. What the shop sells, since the site links a store but the pricing was not in the pages we read.

Every figure here is Moonwell's own, read September 29, 2026, and none is independently measured. A live widget is a self-report.

## The judgement

Moonwell is the answer to "which TBC realm should I actually try", and the answer is the x5 realm, with the caveat that it is a quarter of its July size. Fifteen years of continuous operation, dated phase releases, a 10-player Zul'Aman, and a client you already own are a more durable proposition than almost anything new in this scene.

Two cautions. The new core is the real risk, and it is being handled correctly. And the entry we had for this realm was wrong in a way that would have sent readers to the five-player side of a server with 854 people on it, which is the argument for working the stale queue rather than trusting the file.
