## TL;DR

- Sam Didier defined Warcraft's look across 30 years: chunky silhouettes, primary palettes, instant heroism.
- His rules were functional, not decorative: top-down readability, 16-color discipline, faction color language.
- Tomorrow's reveal should be judged by those rules, not polygon counts. The full weekend schedule is in the [BlizzCon preview](/news/blizzcon-2026-preview/).

One day before BlizzCon, the most useful Classic+ document is not a leak. It is a year-old interview with the man who decided what Warcraft looks like.

MadMadeLabel's long conversation with Sam "Samwise" Didier covers thirty years at Blizzard, from a classified ad reading only "make art for video games" to the Pandaren joke that became canon. Read it as a spec sheet for Saturday.

## Rule 1: readability beats realism

Blizzard tried realistic proportions for Warcraft III. From the top-down camera it flopped — small, confusing units. Didier's fix was to bulk everything up: thick limbs, oversized weapons, giant shoulder pads. A unit had to read in a split-second glance mid-battle.

The roots go deeper. On the SNES the team had 16 colors per 16-by-16 square, one reserved for transparency. Characters became big, chunky, few-colored shapes because the hardware demanded it. The Lost Vikings went from dozens of puny indistinguishable runners to three readable archetypes.

This is the standard to hold any Classic+ graphics option against. The open community question — retail graphics with a Classic toggle, or one unified look — is really a Samwise question. If the new look stays readable at a glance, it is Warcraft. If it needs a toggle to feel like Warcraft, something failed.

## Rule 2: color is a language

Orcs are green, trolls were green too — same height, same silhouette, unreadable. So in Warcraft III trolls became blue jungle trolls, tauren brown and tan, and everyone got team red. Faction identity in primaries: Horde reds, Alliance blues, Legion greens.

Detail followed the same discipline. Five spikes on a shoulder pad become two or three scaled up. Three skulls on a belt become one giant skull moved where you can see it. Anything too small turns to mush, so it gets omitted or enlarged. No exceptions for realism.

## Rule 3: level 1 should look like level 5

Didier's characters radiate heroism before you click Start. The Orc Blademaster began as his own D&D half-orc, Grumoq the Wanderer, then got pushed into crazier territory: war banner, braided ponytail with a blade, beaded necklace, oversized curved sword. Instant history, worn on the model.

Even the name Warcraft came from tabletop — his Fighter, where witches used witchcraft and the warrior used Warcraft. The IP's title is a D&D joke that stuck.

## The collaboration that built the Horde

The famous orc split with Chris Metzen took about two seconds to resolve. Metzen's brawlers became the leaders — Thrall, Grom — while Didier's monstrous brutes became the grunts, wolf riders, and rank and file. Two visions, no winner picked, both shipped. It is worth remembering when fandom treats every design choice as a war: the Horde itself is a compromise.

Same with the 2D-to-3D leap. Same team, same style, one method: keep it super simple, plaster low-poly models with hand-drawn silhouette textures, let paint carry what polygons could not. Didier personally textured over a third of the Warcraft III cast.

## Why this matters Saturday

Stylization ages; realism dates. Didier compares the old games to early Disney — Snow White and Bambi still resonate because they never chased realism. A Classic+ reveal chasing modern fidelity at the cost of silhouette and palette would break the one continuity the franchise has never lost.

Watch the title card, then the first gameplay frame, and run the Samwise checklist: does it read at a glance, does the palette speak faction, does a level 1 look like trouble? The [Warcraft Forever name trail](/blog/warcraft-forever-triple-signal/) tells us what it might be called. Samwise tells us what it has to look like.

## FAQ

**Is this interview new?**
No — September 2025. It is linked here as context for the reveal, not news.

**Did Samwise confirm anything about Classic+?**
Nothing. He left Blizzard years ago. This is philosophy, not intel.

**What is the "pee pee tile"?**
A 16-by-16 Warcraft II winter tile with his name in yellow pixels, usable by map makers. Easter eggs were open culture, not secrets.

**Who influenced the influencer?**
His top five: Larry Elmore, John Buscema, Bernie Wrightson, Albert Uderzo, and Rankin/Bass animation — plus KISS and Iron Maiden covers. None of which his art resembles directly, which is the point.
