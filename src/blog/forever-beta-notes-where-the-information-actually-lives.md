We publish a lot of Forever material, and a fair question from a reader is which of it is worth reading. This is a ranked map of the sources, with the checkable content in each named.

## 1. Class deep dives: the densest material anywhere

**Highest value by a wide margin**, because they contain numbers.

Level gates, baseline ability lists, talent milestone positions, pet families. The 30 September posts for **Hunter and Druid** and for **Priest and Warrior** are the only first-party Forever documents that let you argue with a design.

Read them for: **Shadow Word: Death at level 32**, the **16-point talent milestone**, and which abilities moved from talents to baseline.

Limit: four of ten classes, and Blizzard says the numbers will move.

## 2. The Legacy System post: one number decides it

The **23 September** breakdown is the second densest, and it turns on a single figure: **16 Legacy Points per character at launch**.

Sixty-five challenges across six categories, points account-wide, perks in three trees. Read it for the cap and for **Well Rested**, and note that **Legacy Rewards is named and never described**, which is the largest gap in Blizzard's own documentation of this system.

## 3. The named explainers: useful, thinner than they look

Blizzard's Wow Weekly on **25 September** indexed six of them: choose your ruleset, name your character, create your hero, the Skyborne, the Legacy System, and podcast episode 1.

The naming explainer is worth reading for one sentence: character names get a **two-name structure** for "a larger, realmless Azeroth". **Realmless** is the single most consequential word Blizzard has published about Forever's structure and it appears in a naming post.

## 4. Our CDN watcher: precise, and not about content

Build numbers, sequences, and the config fields that let us tell a genuinely new build from a CDN re-point. Four builds in five days, all four regions in parity across six pushes.

Read it for: **whether a build is real**, and **whether a region is behind**. It cannot tell you what changed and we will never claim otherwise.

## 5. Fan references: behind, and one is useful

All three references we track sit on **70009**, the last build any of them wrote about, from 25 September. That is four builds and about forty hours stale.

The exception is **classicwowforever**, which published genuine notes for 70009: two talents cut, two renamed, Wrath and Holy Strike buffs, a flat Eureka! discount, easier campfires, quest fixes. That is the kind of writing worth having and it needs catching up.

## 6. Retail coverage: adjacent, and dated

**Midnight's 12.1.5 arrives 13 October**, three weeks before Forever launches. Retail uses **Mythic**, **Season**, **Keystone** and a **Tier 11 solo** challenge. Forever publishes **Legacy talents**, a **16-point milestone**, **65 challenges** and **Dual Specialization at 40**.

Two design vocabularies, three weeks apart, from one studio. Useful context, not the same game.

## The gap worth knowing about

**There is no public hotfix channel for Forever.**

Blizzard's **October 1 hotfix post** covers Midnight, MoP Classic, Season of Discovery, TBC Classic, Classic Era and Hardcore. **Forever is not in the list**, and that is expected, because the beta runs on its own manifest and has a separate tester feedback channel.

The consequence is real: a player who wants to know what was fixed has to read beta development notes or ask a tester Discord. **There is no equivalent of a retail hotfix log for this branch**, and we do not know of a public one. That is a gap in the information a player can get, not evidence that no fixes are shipping.

## What we would read first, in order

The **Legacy System post** (one number, one guide to write), then the **Priest and Warrior deep dive** (the most concrete class document), then **classicwowforever's 70009 notes** (the last real written account of a client).

And on launch day itself, our **build report**, which will be the freshest Forever information anywhere and will tell you almost nothing.
