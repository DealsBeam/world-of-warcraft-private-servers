If you play on a private server, you are almost certainly in voice chat more than you are in retail. Guild raids run on Discord or TeamSpeak, roleplay servers run on it all night, and a lot of private server communities are small enough that the voice channel *is* the server. Audio is the one purchase that changes how the game feels, and it is the one most people get wrong by buying the wrong thing.

## What actually matters on a private server

**A separate microphone beats a headset boom arm, almost every time.** A cheap condenser on a desk stand, close to your mouth, sounds better than an expensive headset with a boom arm clipped to the side of your head, because a headset mic is far from your mouth and picks up your keyboard. If you can only buy one thing, buy the mic.

**Position beats specification.** A $50 microphone at fifteen centimetres sounds better than a $200 one at half a metre. Distance and a pop filter do more work than any spec sheet.

**USB is fine until it is not.** USB microphones are plug and play, which is what a private server player wants, since you are often moving between a gaming machine and a laptop. XLR needs an interface, more cables, and gain settings. If you already own an interface, use it. If you do not, do not buy one for a microphone.

**Wireless is a latency decision, not a quality decision.** A good wireless headset sounds fine. It also occasionally drops a tenth of a second, which you notice more in a raid than you notice anything in the music.

## What to skip

**Theatre surround headsets.** A headset advertising 7.1 or Dolby Atmos gives you virtual surround that a private server raid does not use, and it often costs you the thing you wanted, which is a clean microphone.

**Bluetooth headsets.** Compressed audio and a visible lip sync gap, which is exactly the delay you notice while someone is calling a pull.

**A cheap boom arm before a cheap microphone.** An arm on a $20 mic just puts the mic further from your mouth.

**A $60 interface for a $60 microphone.** It adds a gain knob and a problem.

## The part that is not a purchase

If your guild sounds like everyone is talking through a bucket, the fix is usually not hardware. It is push to talk instead of voice activation, a noise suppression setting in your voice app, and muting when you are not speaking. Those three settings cost nothing and do more for a private server voice night than any microphone on this page.
