The first week of the World of Warcraft: Forever beta produced more player-behavior signal than the entire eight months of leaks before it. The reason is a single Reddit census, carried by [Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow-forever/news/wow-forevers-beta-population-is-already-showing-some-clear-favorite-race-class-combos/): on September 20, a community member counted level-20 characters on the PvE ruleset and got numbers that answer a question leaks never could. Which of the new race and class combinations do people actually want to play?

## The numbers

- **1,173 Undead characters, about 63% of them Paladins.**
- **High Order** is the second most popular Alliance race. **Windshaper** is the second most popular Horde race.
- **46% of Dwarves are Shamans.**

Read our own [news post](/news/forever-beta-pve-census-race-class-favorites/) for the full caveat list, because it matters: this is one ruleset, one level band, one day, and a self-reported count. It is not a launch forecast and should never be quoted as one.

## What the signal actually says

The honest reading is that the three freshest combos are the draw, and the magnitude tracks novelty rather than settled preference. Undead Paladin is the newest class in the game and wins by a two-to-one margin. Dwarf Shaman is one of two new class-race pairings and takes nearly half the Dwarf pool. The Skyborne are a paid race with a brand-new starting zone, and they break into the top two on both sides.

That is exactly what you would expect a beta to produce, and it is also exactly what you would not expect a mature launch population to look like. By November, the Undead Paladin share will fall as players finish testing the combo and move on. The durable question is whether the Skyborne hold their top-two placement once the starting zone novelty wears off, and that is not answerable from a level-20 census.

The genuinely new information is the *shape* of demand. Three of the six new race-class combinations Blizzard announced are showing up in a top-two or majority position within a week. The new combos are not curiosities testers queue once and abandon. They are the thing testers log in for.

## Why this matters for the class debate

The census arrives while the class community is arguing about whether the new abilities are balanced. That argument is mostly unanswerable from tooltips, and the tracker has said so repeatedly. The census does not settle balance either. It does something different: it tells us which classes people are actually creating, which is the input a balance discussion usually lacks.

The Battle Mage discovery is the clearest example. A single tester found a scroll-decoding chain that produces the Coldflame Saber, and the same Icy census article links it as something the beta population might yet be pushed toward. Two sources now report it ([our post](/news/forever-beta-battle-mage-comprehension-saber/)). The discovery is reported as a curiosity, not a confirmed archetype, and a frozen-target bonus on one item is not a playstyle. But the fact that a community is independently hunting for arcane-flavored Mage options while the census shows Mages being created in bulk is a signal about where attention is going.

## The private-server parallel

Our own tracker exists because player demand for specific versions of World of Warcraft is a measurable thing, not a vague one. The Forever census is that same measurement, applied to an official launch for the first time. When the 140 servers in our [tracker](/) are sorted by what people actually queue for, the pattern is consistent: players pick the version that matches how they remember the game, and they overwhelmingly pick the one with the most *new* things to do inside the version they remember.

That is the read worth carrying into launch. The combos are the draw now. The question that matters in November is whether the horizontal content, the three-season cadence, and the fixed level 60 hold that draw once the launch spike passes. The census cannot answer it. It can tell us what to watch.

## What to watch next

- Whether the Undead Paladin share falls as beta players finish testing the combo, or holds through launch week.
- Whether Skyborne stay in the top two per faction once the paid-pack novelty effect wears off.
- Whether Dwarf Shaman holds near 46% or settles into a normal class distribution.

The [beta verification hit list](/news/forever-beta-verification-hit-list/) tracks the claims behind all of this. The population data is a signal, not a fact about launch, and we will keep grading it that way.
