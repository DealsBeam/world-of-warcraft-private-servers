The Blackmaw Hold gold farm was one of the biggest stories of Forever's first two weeks. A week later it has a clear official record, and the distinction Blizzard drew matters more than the punishment itself.

On **November 17 at 18:38 UTC**, Kaivax posted "Actions For Blackmaw Hold Abuse" on the official forums. This is what it said, what happened before it, and who is actually at risk.

## What the bug was

Blizzard's own description is precise:

> Over the past weekend, some players discovered a large group of non-elite enemies in the Blackmaw Hold dungeon that were incorrectly dropping the full amount of dungeon loot that would be expected from an elite.

A group of non-elite mobs was paying elite-level loot. That made one spot in one dungeon far more lucrative than any other gold source in the game. The team says it addressed this with a hotfix "early yesterday", which puts the fix on November 16.

## What Blizzard did to accounts

The response had three stages, and they were applied to different groups:

| Group | Action |
|---|---|
| Anyone who ran Blackmaw Hold a few times and benefited | No action, explicitly |
| A couple hundred players who farmed the group exclusively | Economy lock on the account |
| Significant abusers, after investigation | Gold removal and account suspensions |

The economy lock went in first. Blizzard says it was to prevent broader damage to the game economy while it worked out the scale of the gains, including gold earned by vendoring the bugged drops. The gold removal and suspensions followed on November 17.

That ordering is worth understanding. An economy lock is a containment step, not the final penalty. The final penalty was taking the gold and suspending accounts.

## The distinction that matters

The post draws a bright line between accidental and intentional benefit:

> Players who simply ran Blackmaw Hold a few times and benefited organically from the extra loot did nothing wrong, and no one should worry that they're putting their account at risk if they accidentally benefit from a bug.

That is the part most useful to a normal player. Blizzard is not punishing people for running a dungeon while a bug was live. It is punishing people who recognised the bug and built their entire farming routine around it.

## Why the economy response was fast

Kaivax framed the action as part of normal economy management:

> Finding efficient farming spots has always been part of the game, and is how most players have earned their epic mounts since 2004. When a single location proves so lucrative that it overshadows all other sources, we generally adjust it to bring it in line with the rest.

That is the reasoning behind both the hotfix and the account action. A single location that dwarfs everything else is treated as a threat to the wider economy, not just as a bug to patch quietly.

## What we are not claiming

We do not have the exact number of suspended accounts. Blizzard said "a couple hundred" accounts received economy locks and that suspensions applied to "significant abusers" without a count. We have not seen the suspension notices, and we are not estimating.

We also cannot tell you how much gold was removed. The post names the category, vendoring bugged drops, but gives no total.
