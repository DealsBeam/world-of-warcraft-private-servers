**World of Warcraft: Forever launches 4 November 2026 at 3:00 p.m. Pacific**, which is **23:00 UTC** because US Pacific daylight time ends on 1 November and launch is three days after it.

That is **33 days from this post**. Below is a ranked list of what we think will go wrong on the day, in order of how much it will cost a player. We have put the things we have evidence for above the things we are guessing about, because that ordering is the whole point.

## 1. Login queues, and the error they produce

A launch is a thundering herd and every launch-day server has one. What makes it worse than normal is that Forever is **realmless by default**, per Blizzard's own naming post, which describes "a larger, realmless Azeroth". Realmless removes the usual mitigation of picking a quiet shard, because there is not a set of shards to pick from.

**What we do not know:** whether launch-day capacity was provisioned for one global queue or many. Nobody has published it.

**Cost if wrong:** hours, possibly a day of play lost.

## 2. The talent gates, if 16 points is still 16

The Legacy System caps spending at **16 points per character at launch**. That number is on the record from 23 September and it is the design's load-bearing constraint.

The launch risk is not the cap itself, it is whether the cap moves. Blizzard's own deep dives warn systems "may continue to evolve throughout the beta test", and a cap that tight is exactly the kind of number that gets loosened after feedback. If it changes, every guide written before launch is wrong, including ours.

**Cost if wrong:** your plan for the first week is wrong, not your character.

## 3. Baseline abilities arriving at levels you expect them at

**Shadow Word: Death at level 32** is the clearest case, and there are more: Aimed Shot at 20, Fear Ward and Devouring Plague at 20, Divine Spirit at 30, and Blessing of Kings plus Improved Mark of the Wild demoted from talents to baseline.

Six of ten classes have **no published deep dive**. The structural change, the 16-point milestone, is currently specified for four classes, so half the game is undescribed and the described half may have moved.

**Cost if wrong:** you relevel a character or respec a talent tree. Low cost, high annoyance.

## 4. Addons, and we mean yours

This is the one we would warn a reader hardest about, and the evidence is unusually explicit. Blizzard's own addon ecosystem and our own beta observations both point the same way: **Forever rewrites combat, classes, abilities, loot, inventory and the interface**, and the stated position is that addons from other servers may not work.

Torment, which we added to the tracker yesterday, makes the same point about itself in almost identical language, and it is not a Blizzard property. That is what a full-rewrite client does to an addon ecosystem.

**Cost if wrong:** your WeakAura setup, your ElvUI, your DBM. For a player who has spent fifteen years on those, this is the most expensive item on the list.

## 5. The published record will be useless on launch day

This one is about us and the rest of the coverage. The last Forever build with written notes from anyone we track is **70009**, from 25 September, and four builds have shipped since. Our watcher will tell you a build number on launch morning. **Nobody will be able to tell you what broke**, because the failure modes a launch produces are exactly the things a manifest does not record.

**Cost if wrong:** you spend launch day reading stale notes, as we all will.

## 6. Timezones, which is the one we have got wrong before

Blizzard's launch announcement says **3:00 p.m. PDT** and the What's Next recap says **PST**. We checked the arithmetic on 29 September rather than deferring to either, and confirmed that **PST is correct**: daylight time ends 1 November, launch is 4 November, so Pacific is on standard time and 3 p.m. is **23:00 UTC**.

A source disagreeing with us is a reason to check the arithmetic, not a reason to defer. If you see both labels out there, they cannot both be right and we have published which one is.

**Cost if wrong:** one hour. Listed here because we nearly got it wrong and readers should know the check was done.

## What we are not ranking

**Server population.** Blizzard's numbers, whatever they are, will be for the client and not for the private servers. We will be publishing the private-server readings on launch day, from the operators' own widgets, labelled as self-reported, which is a completely different number and we will not conflate the two.

## What would change this list

Anything in the next 33 days that is a **dated, first-party statement**: a hotfix channel, the remaining six class deep dives, a capacity announcement, a Legacy Rewards explanation. All four would move an item on this list, and three of them are gaps in Blizzard's own documentation rather than things we have failed to find.
