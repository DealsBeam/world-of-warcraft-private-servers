Official TBC Anniversary did what private TBC has been doing for a decade: it shipped Black Temple and Hyjal again. On August 27 the phase opened with the two raids, the Netherwing daily chain, epic gems and the next grind tier for the Anniversary line. For anyone tracking the private scene that is less a headline than a benchmark, because the same content lives on five or six tracked realms right now — [Moonwell](/servers/moonwell/), [Faebright](/servers/faebright/), [Whitemane — Sunwell](/servers/whitemane-sunwell/), [Rising Gods — Back2Basics](/servers/rising-gods-back2basics/), [BloodWoW (TBC)](/servers/bloodwow-tbc/) and [Tirisgarde](/servers/tirisgarde/) — with very different answers to the same tuning questions.

## The official tuning as a data point

The Anniversary phase shipped as a clean progression slice. Two raids, one rep, a gem tier, no new catch-up beyond what the previous phase had already normalized. Attunements mattered again in the way they stopped mattering on most high-rate private realms years ago. When you see an official re-release run the content at its original size and gates and let players hit the wall at a familiar pace, you learn a few things about why some private versions feel too fast and some feel too grindy.

On high-rate or instant-70 realms the same wall does not exist. You blast through the pre-quests, you buy the crafted set, you raid the first week. That is the design. On 1x or low-rate realms like Moonwell's veteran 2.4.3 or Rising Gods' long-running german 2.4.3, the wall is there on purpose, and the question the Anniversary launch answers is: how long does Blizzard think the wall should take when the game around it is otherwise modern (achievements, collection log, trading post-adjacent systems)? The answer in August 2026 is: about the same as 2007.

## What private TBC already knew

Three private-server patterns that the official phase re-validated:

**Hyjal trash still gates more than bosses.** Every TBC server that runs 2.4.3 at 2-3x has the same note in its changelog: Hyjal wave tuning, trash respawn, trash skip exploits. It is the only raid in the expansion where the corridor is the hard part. The Anniversary launch did not change the waves. Private realms that did are now visibly different from the official line.

**Netherwing as a population engine.** The Anniversary team shipped Netherwing as the post-raid daily loop. Private servers do the same, but with smaller populations the loop often becomes the whole game. Moonwell's 70-100 online has to make the same dailies work when the competition for eggs is five people, not fifty. That is a different design problem than the one Blizzard just solved.

**Epic gems as an economy tell.** Anniversary gems entered as a gold sink and an auction-house magnet. On private TBC with no paywall, the same gems become a gold check on how healthy the economy is. Warlords of Draenor-style mission-table gold or a broken auction bot shows up first in the gem price, not in the raid log.

## Where the tracker sits

We have 7 TBC entries. 5 playable, 1 in development (Whitemane Sunwell, the TBC leg of the Whitemane network, promising a fresh 2.4.3 realm on the same infrastructure), 1 dead (Felmyst, offline since 2023). Of the 5 playable, 2 are small (Moonwell, Rising Gods), 3 are tiny. None are large. TBC as a lived game is small and specific: 18-year infrastructure, low-rate community servers, a German institution, a solo-friendly bot realm, a 1x veteran.

None of that will change because official TBC shipped Phase 3. But the new phase does answer the question the tracker hears most about TBC private servers — "is this just doing what official already does?" — with: yes, for this patch window, the two tracks are doing the same two raids for the same two reasons. The difference is the track underneath. Private TBC has had to solve the small-population version of the same content. Watching how official re-tuned it for a medium population answers whether private's answers were too generous, too stingy, or just right.

For now, the official version is the reference. Private TBC is the variation. Until the next phase lands, we treat the Anniversary build as the patch note private TBC did not ship.
