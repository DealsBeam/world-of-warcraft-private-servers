Official TBC Anniversary did what private TBC has done for a decade: it shipped Black Temple and Hyjal again.

On Aug 27 the phase opened with two raids, Netherwing dailies, epic gems and the next grind tier.

For anyone tracking private, that is less headline than benchmark.

The same content lives on five tracked realms — [Moonwell](/servers/moonwell/), [Faebright](/servers/faebright/) and [Whitemane — Sunwell](/servers/whitemane-sunwell/).

Also [Rising Gods — Back2Basics](/servers/rising-gods-back2basics/), [BloodWoW (TBC)](/servers/bloodwow-tbc/) and [Tirisgarde](/servers/tirisgarde/) — with different tuning answers.

## The official tuning as a data point

Anniversary shipped a clean slice. Two raids, one rep, one gem tier, no new catch-up beyond what Phase 2 normalized.

Attunements mattered again in the way they stopped mattering on most high-rate private realms.

When official runs the content at original size and gates, you learn why some private versions feel too fast and some too grindy.

On high-rate or instant-70 the wall does not exist. You blast pre-quests, buy crafted, raid week one. That is the design.

On 1x or low-rate — Moonwell's veteran 2.4.3, Rising Gods' German 2.4.3 — the wall is on purpose.

The question Anniversary answers: how long should the wall take when the game around it is modern?

Answer in Aug 2026: about the same as 2007.

## What private TBC already knew

Three patterns the official phase re-validated:

**Hyjal trash still gates more than bosses.** Every 2.4.3 at 2-3x has the same changelog note: wave tuning, respawn, skip exploits.

Hyjal is the only raid where the corridor is the hard part. Anniversary did not change the waves.

Private realms that did are now visibly different from official.

**Netherwing as a population engine.** Anniversary shipped it as the post-raid daily loop.

Private does the same, but with smaller pops the loop often becomes the whole game.

Moonwell's 70-100 has to make the same dailies work when egg competition is five, not fifty. Different design problem than Blizzard's.

**Epic gems as an economy tell.** Anniversary gems are a gold sink and auction magnet.

On private with no paywall, the same gems become a gold check.

Warlords-style mission-table gold or a broken AH bot shows first in gem price, not raid log.

## Where the tracker sits

We have 7 TBC entries. 5 playable, 1 in dev (Whitemane Sunwell), 1 dead (Felmyst, offline since 2023).

Of the 5 playable, 2 are small (Moonwell, Rising Gods), 3 are tiny. None are large.

TBC as a lived game is small and specific: 18-year infra, low-rate community, a German institution, a bot-friendly solo realm, a 1x veteran.

None of that changes because official shipped Phase 3.

But the new phase answers the question we hear most — "is this just doing what official already does?"

For this window, yes: same two raids for same two reasons.

Difference is the track underneath. Private has solved the small-pop version of the same content.

Watching how official re-tuned it for medium pop answers whether private was too generous, too stingy, or just right.

For now, official is the reference. Private is the variation.

Until next phase, treat the Anniversary build as the patch note private TBC did not ship.
