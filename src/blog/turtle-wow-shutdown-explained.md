Turtle WoW was the biggest custom "Classic+" private server in the world — a Vanilla realm with custom zones, classes, and content layered onto the classic client. It ran for nearly eight years, built a devoted community, and even had its own in-game radio station. On May 15, 2026, it closed permanently.

This is the story of how that happened, and what rose from the wreckage.

## How it ended

Turtle WoW shut down after losing a major federal copyright infringement lawsuit in California: *Blizzard Entertainment, Inc. v. Turtle WoW*.

The case was notable for how Blizzard prosecuted it. The complaint used the **RICO Act** against individual developers and community managers — framing the server as an organized enterprise rather than a fan project. Blizzard also aggressively subpoenaed Discord, hosting providers, and payment processors to build its case, cutting off the infrastructure the community depended on.

After the court ruled in Blizzard's favor on April 13, 2026, Turtle WoW agreed to a binding permanent injunction and permanently closed its servers. The era was over.

## The irony: a leak had already guaranteed Turtle's survival

The strange part is that Turtle WoW was already "immortal" before it died. In September 2024, a comprehensive leak published the server's proprietary core and client code up to version 1.17 — the same code Blizzard's case was ultimately about. The leak was mirrored across GitHub and RaGEZONE, and it effectively democratized years of custom Classic+ development.

That leak is exactly why the Turtle community had somewhere to go.

## Where the community went

When Turtle closed, its ecosystem fragmented into a family of Vanilla+ projects — most of them running Turtle's own 1.17/1.18 code:

- **[Octo WoW](/servers/octo-wow/)** — restored the Turtle 1.18.1 client in July 2026 with three realms (N'Zoth PvE, C'Thun hardcore, Y'Shaarj PvP) and phased raids through 2026-27. Also the home of the [modern OctoWoW client](/guides/octowow/).
- **[Capybara WoW](/servers/capybara-wow/)** — run by ex-Turtle SEA admins, two realms, roughly 10k online.
- **[Ravencraft](/servers/ravencraft/)** — a Turtle 1.18.1 continuation by former project contributors, launching **August 22, 2026**. [More on the launch](/news/ravencraft-launch/).
- **[Caverns of Time](/servers/caverns-of-time/)** — a Turtle+ variant that launched August 15, 2026.
- **[SandWorlds](/servers/sandworlds/)** — a Brazilian Vanilla+ realm with 13 custom zones and cross-faction play.

Beyond the Turtle clones, the wider Vanilla+ scene kept running: [Project Legacy](/servers/project-legacy/) and [Incursion-WoW](/servers/incursion-wow/) both offer independent takes, and [Ascension](/servers/ascension/) continues its classless seasons despite its own legal fight.

## The radio survived too

One of the few pieces of Turtle WoW to outlive the server itself was its in-game radio. Everlook Broadcasting Co., tuned via the minimap, kept broadcasting after the shutdown — rebranding as Out of Bounds Entertainment and streaming around the clock. The in-game tuner died with the server, but the station lives on, and you can [listen to it here](/radio/).

## What Turtle's death means for the scene

Turtle's shutdown is the clearest example yet of Blizzard's 2026 legal escalation — the full story is in [our crackdown explainer](/blog/blizzard-2026-private-server-crackdown/). The pattern it set (RICO, infrastructure subpoenas, permanent injunctions) now hangs over every major private server.

But the counter-pattern matters too: the 2024 leak turned a single server into a *genre*. Turtle WoW is gone; Turtle-style Vanilla+ play has never been more alive, with [Octo WoW](/servers/octo-wow/) and [Capybara](/servers/capybara-wow/) carrying real populations and RavenCraft launching this week. Blizzard closed the original. The idea outran the injunction.
