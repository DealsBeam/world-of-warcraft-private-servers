Two networks dominate the top of [the tracker](/) by size, and their names are easy to confuse. **Warmane** is the older, single-expansion giant. **Whitemane** is the newer network that runs several expansions at once. Five of the eleven realms we rate large are Whitemane-branded, and Warmane is the other long-standing name at the top. Here is the honest comparison.

## The short answer

| If you want | Pick |
| --- | --- |
| The largest, longest-running WotLK scene | **Warmane** |
| A fresh, modern-client Wrath realm | **Whitemane Frostmourne** |
| Several expansions, one account and one launcher | **Whitemane** |
| Native Mac, Linux, or Steam Deck support | **Whitemane** |
| A 1x blizzlike Wrath journey | **Warmane Lordaeron** |
| Progressive Cataclysm or Mists | **Whitemane Gilneas, Maelstrom, Stormstout** |

## Warmane: the incumbent

Warmane traces its roots to Molten-WoW and has been the default answer to "where do I play Wrath" for years. It runs **four WotLK realm boards, including a progressive Onyxia**, alongside Lordaeron for 1x play, Icecrown for high-rate play, and Blackrock for PvP.

What it has going for it:

- **Scale and age.** It is one of the largest and oldest WotLK operations in the world, and its community is correspondingly deep.
- **A real devlog and changelog.** Its own changelog runs to hundreds of lines with dated bug numbers, and its devlog entries read like an operator actually working on a live game. One entry reworked the Wintergrasp queue to stop multiboxers holding multiple of the 120 spots, which is the kind of specific fix you want to see.
- **Its client is now part of the ecosystem.** Other live servers redistribute Warmane's 3.3.5a client as a supported download, which tells you how established it is. That story is in [the client you did not build](/blog/the-client-you-did-not-build/).

What to know:

- **Its news block is a stale archive.** This is a documented quirk, not a sign of life or death. We learned that the hard way: on September 29 2026 we first published Warmane as an unverified liveness claim and corrected ourselves the same day, because a missing registration page proves nothing about whether a server runs. The full correction is recorded in the entry and in [our correction policy](/blog/tracker-correction-policy-explained/).
- **It is a WotLK answer, mostly.** If you want to play Cataclysm, Mists, or TBC right now, Warmane is not the network for it.

## Whitemane: the network

Whitemane has been running since **2020** and describes itself as free to play with no pay-to-win. Its distinguishing feature in 2026 is breadth: it runs WotLK, Cataclysm, Mists of Pandaria, and a TBC realm in development, all under one account, and it ships a **launcher for Windows, Mac, Linux, and Steam Deck**.

- **[Frostmourne](/servers/whitemane-frostmourne/)** is the headline: a WotLK+ realm on the 3.3.5a line with Heroic Naxxramas, personal loot, collections, Vulkan support, HD models, class changes, and a level 19 twink scene. It launched September 19 2026 with a peak above **8,500 players**, weathered a 30-minute DDoS outage at launch, and has been stable since. This is the freshest content on either network.
- **[Gilneas](/servers/whitemane-gilneas/)** and **[Maelstrom](/servers/whitemane-maelstrom/)** run Cataclysm 4.3.4 with QoL features, custom legendaries, solo-queue PvP, and live rated battleground matchmaking.
- **[Stormstout](/servers/whitemane-stormstout/)** is the Mists of Pandaria 5.4.8 realm, and it is the caveat: its beta is online but a full launch was delayed by a few months, per a June 30 news post.
- **[Sunwell](/servers/whitemane-sunwell/)** is the TBC 2.4.3 realm, listed as coming soon and still in development.

What to know:

- **Breadth is the selling point, and also the risk.** Running four eras is more surface area than one, and Stormstout and Sunwell are not finished. Frostmourne and the two Cataclysm realms are the parts you can play today.
- **The launcher genuinely lowers the entry cost.** A single launcher across operating systems is unusual in this scene, and it is the reason Whitemane comes up in handheld conversations. See our [Steam Deck and handheld guide](/blog/wow-private-servers-steam-deck-handhelds/).

## Head to head

- **Age and stability:** Warmane, clearly. It has survived the scene's entire modern history.
- **Freshness:** Whitemane, clearly. Frostmourne opened in September 2026; Warmane's newest boards are a progressive Onyxia rather than a new realm.
- **Era coverage:** Whitemane, by a wide margin.
- **Client convenience:** Whitemane, for the cross-platform launcher.
- **Monetisation stance:** both describe themselves as not pay-to-win, and both run shops. See [who sells power and who sells cosmetics](/blog/donor-gear-policies-compared-oct-9/) for the wider picture.
- **Transparency:** Warmane publishes a devlog and changelog; Whitemane's own news dates its delays, which is also to its credit.

## Which one

- **Play Warmane** if you want the largest, most settled Wrath population, a mature 1x realm, or the long-tail PvP of Blackrock, and you are content to stay in Wrath.
- **Play Whitemane** if you want a fresh Wrath realm on the modern line, you want Cataclysm or (soon) Mists and TBC under one account, or you play on Mac, Linux, or a handheld.
- **You do not have to choose.** They are different accounts and different clients, and many players run both. What you should not do is assume the bigger brand is the right era for you.

Both entries carry their own dates and caveats on [the tracker](/), and we would rather you read those than our summary. Population bands stay as recorded: large for the realms above, unknown for any project that publishes no counter.
